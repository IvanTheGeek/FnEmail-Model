## 1. The Role Catalog

The kit's form is Name / Description / Key actions / **Permissions boundary** — "What can this role NOT do?" (`SKILL.md:380-384`). The *Cannot* column is the one that keeps getting dropped, so it is filled for every row.

RFC 5321 is unusual for a role catalog: **it names almost no humans.** The document is written about hosts and processes. The humans are there — postmaster, list administrator, site operator, spammer — but they appear at the edges, in the security and configuration text, never in the protocol proper. That asymmetry is itself a Step 1 finding, and it predicts where Step 3 storyboarding will find no screen to draw.

| Attacker | "there has been an increase of attacks on SMTP servers, either in conjunction with attempts to discover addresses for sending unsolicited messages or simply to make the servers inaccessible to others" | §7.8, 4341-4345 | An adversarial actor the RFC models explicitly. ### Notes on the catalog

- **Every human role here would be excluded by at least one of this project's screens.** Postmaster and Mailbox Owner fall to the responsibility-boundary cutoff; Site Operator falls to the Infrastructure altitude tier; List Administrator and Attacker fall to the HELO-only charter and the inbound-only scope; Implementer and IANA fall to every screen at once. The kit's gate is blunter than any of them: "Do not proceed until the Role Catalog exists" (`METHOD-REFERENCE-DETAIL.md:205`), and Step 3 demands "one swimlane per human role" (`SKILL.md:417`). A model with zero human roles has no storyboard.
- **Client and server are role-relative, not host-relative.** §2.3.2, 651-653: "Since a given host may act both as server and client in a relay situation, "receiver" and "sender" terminology is still used where needed for clarity." A relay is both. Any catalog that assigns a host one row is already wrong.
- **The MTA/MUA boundary is disclaimed by the RFC itself** (§2.3.3, 667-682). Treat those two rows as vocabulary, not as a partition.

## 2. Chapters (timelines)

Terminology note, so the assembler is not surprised: the kit says **chapter** / **timeline**; this project settled on **workflow** for a named group of slices — Dymitruk's word 241 times to 10, *chapter* being Dilger's (`docs/DECISIONS.md` line 94). That ruling is not reopened here. The chapters below are named in the kit's vocabulary because this is a Step 1 deliverable; rename them workflows on the way in.

The kit's test for a group, all three of which each chapter below is checked against: "Represents a single, coherent business process", "Can be understood as a standalone narrative on its own", "Would be naturally owned by one team or one domain expert" (`SKILL.md:140-142`). And the restraint: "If all events belong to a single flow, one timeline is correct — do not split artificially" (`SKILL.md:144`).

### Two candidates deliberately not made chapters

- **Extension Negotiation.** §2.2 (478), §2.2.2 (532), §4.1.1.1 (1783). While the model is HELO-only, this is three or four events inside Session Lifecycle and splitting it would be the artificial split `SKILL.md:144` forbids. If ESMTP ever enters scope, promote it: the negotiation, the per-verb parameters (§4.1.1.11, 2228), and the §2.2.3 requeue-on-unsupported behavior are a standalone narrative with one owner.
- **Extension and Parameter Registration.** §2.2.2 (532), §8 (4383). A real state change — a registry gains a keyword — with a real actor (IANA/IESG), but the state lives outside any system FnEmail will build. Record it, do not chapter it, unless the model wants to hold registry facts.

### Coverage against the eleven sweeps

The brief's thirteen topics map on with nothing left over: session lifecycle → 1; transaction → 2; content → 3; trace → 4; delivery → 5; relay → 8; gateway → 9; address resolution → 6; list/alias expansion → 10 (+ 11); diagnostics → 13; error and retry handling → 7, 12, 14; configuration and provisioning → 15; security and abuse → 16.

Three chapters have no counterpart in the brief's list and were added from the RFC: **5 Responsibility Handoff** (the 250-after-DATA moment is the single most consequential state change in the document and belongs to neither delivery nor transaction alone), **11 Forwarding and Address Correction** (§3.4's 251/551 machinery is neither relaying nor list expansion), and the split of error handling into **12 Non-Delivery Notification** and **14 Protocol Fault Handling** — a bounce is a new message sent later to a different party, a 500 is a reply inside the current session. One team would not own both.

Chapters 8, 9, 11, 13 and 16 are entirely outside the current charter, and 6, 7, 10 and 12 are mostly outside it. That is expected and is not a reason to drop them: chapters are discovered once, in Step 1, and Step 2 may not invent new ones — "Timelines (chapters) are **created and assigned during Step 1 (Brainstorming)**. This step does not create new chapters" (`METHOD-REFERENCE-DETAIL.md:278`).

## 3. Corpus check

### What the method's Step 1 requires and this brainstorm is not doing

The kit's Step 1 has **six numbered sub-steps** (`SKILL.md:372-510`). The eleven span sweeps plus this task cover sub-step 1 (Roles), part of sub-step 3 (Processes, via chapter discovery) and sub-step 4 (State Changes). **Three are unassigned:**

1. **Sub-step 2 — Identify Event Streams (Stream Roots)** (`SKILL.md:422-432`). Nobody has been asked to name the stream roots or their identity keys. The kit is explicit that these are "NOT DDD aggregates—they're simply the logical roots of events" (`SKILL.md:423-424`), each needing a Name in domain language and an Identity key. For RFC 5321 the obvious candidates are Session, Transaction, Recipient, Message, Queue Entry, Next-Hop Destination and Mailbox — and the choice matters, because whether `RecipientAccepted` belongs to a Recipient stream or a Transaction stream is a real modeling decision that Step 1 is supposed to make. **This is the largest gap.**
2. **Sub-step 5 — Document Business Rules and Constraints** (`SKILL.md:449-457`). Unassigned, and for an RFC-derived model it is the richest seam in the source: the MUST / SHOULD / MAY sentences *are* the business rules, already written down and already numbered. The kit ties them straight to events — the Interview Phase's follow-up trigger for each rule is "When this rule is violated, what event signals that?" (`SKILL.md:42`). Running that question over §4.5.1's minimum command set, §4.1.4's ordering rules and §6.1's post-acceptance obligation would generate events no span is currently looking for.
3. **Sub-step 6 — Create Analysis Document** (`SKILL.md:459-510`). This project has no board. The *substance* behind them — every event in a named chapter — is item 2 above and does apply.

### Is the corpus missing material needed to do Step 1 properly?

**Three real gaps, one of them structural.**

1. **There is no worked Role Catalog anywhere in the corpus except the kit's own illustration.** `SKILL.md:388-414` gives a generic e-commerce example (Customer, Seller, Support Agent, Payment Gateway, Inventory System). A repo-wide search for "role catalog" returns only that, the derivative summaries in `METHOD-REFERENCE-DETAIL.md`, the downstream skills that consume it, and this project's own proposal at `CORRECTIONS-v0.1.md:79`. **No third-party model's real Role Catalog is archived**, so there is nothing to calibrate against — no evidence of how many roles a real catalog holds, how it handles a role that is both human and system, or what a permissions boundary looks like when written by someone who was not writing an example.
2. **The Role Catalog and the chapter/timeline are not Dymitruk's — and the corpus makes that checkable, which is worth stating plainly.** The canonical article's Step 1 is one paragraph (`archive/eventmodeling-org/posts/what-is-event-modeling/index.md:120`) and mentions neither. Its own Step 1 artifact, `Step-1_large.jpg` in the same directory, was opened for this check: it is a loose pile of orange stickies with no lanes, no chapters, no timeline and no actor annotation — legible labels include Payment Requested, Payment Succeeded, Guest Left Hotel, Room Booked, Room Added and Registered. Two consequences. First, **the mandatory Role Catalog and mandatory chapters are the kit's additions**, so the Step 1 gate this task is satisfying is Nebulit's, not Dymitruk's — cite it as such. Second, several stickies carry field pairs (`Lat` and `Long` values) directly on the event, which **corroborates the kit's mandatory-fields rule from Dymitruk's own artifact** — fields at Step 1 are original, not a kit invention.
3. **Nothing archived shows chapter discovery being performed.** The kit states the rule (`SKILL.md:133-187`) and the consequence of skipping it (`SKILL.md:279`) but no worked example shows a real event list being partitioned, or a borderline case being argued. For a domain like SMTP, where relay, gateway and delivery are three named system types doing overlapping work, a worked partition is exactly what would settle whether chapters 5, 8 and 9 above are three chapters or one.

**What is not missing:** the Step 1 rules themselves are complete and unambiguous in the archive — past tense, only facts, immutable, unique semantics, the queries-are-not-events test, fieldless events legitimate, essential fields only, cardinality on every field, no CRUD events, domain language. All are archived with line numbers and none needed to be searched for. Nothing further needs to be fetched or archived to run Step 1; the three gaps above degrade *calibration and provenance*, not the ability to execute.
