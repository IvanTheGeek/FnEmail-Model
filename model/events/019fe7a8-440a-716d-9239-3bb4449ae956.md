## Notes

Inherited (candidate 31, renamed from DuplicateRecipientEliminatedBySourceExpansion to name the attempt rather than a result the RFC says is unreliable). Kept in this chapter because §3.5.4 is one of its RFC anchors and no other chapter claims that section; the closely related MailingListExpandedAtSource (candidate 30) is the expansion itself and belongs to a List Expansion chapter — cross-referenced, not merged, because expanding is not deduplicating and §3.5.4 deprecates only the second. Strength: SHOULD NOT (1416-1417). The stated reason is worth keeping because it names causes that are events elsewhere: MX and CNAME records, local host aliases, and proxying arrangements mean one person reachable at two addresses cannot be recognized as one (1412-1416) — so MailRoutingDnsRecordPublished and AliasEntryCreated are the direct causes of this event's unreliability. Rejected alternative names: SourceExpansionDeduplicationPerformed, DuplicateRecipientEliminatedBySourceExpansion. Marked doubtful: the losing arm of a SHOULD NOT is a weak event, and the RFC describes the practice in the past tense ('Some systems have attempted') rather than prescribing anything. Which screen would have killed it: the inbound-only scope, the responsibility-boundary cutoff, the consumer test (G1). FIELD HYGIENE PASS: nothing struck. No reply code, no invented identity key, and all three values source — the dropped recipient is the third member of the RFC's own list at line 1318, and bar.com is the walk scene's originating host. Note that dropped_recipient was tested against the bar on derived values and kept: it is not a computation the model performs, it is the recipient the originating system actually removed, which is the whole content of the event. expanding_host repeats the actor and is flagged on the same footing as server_host elsewhere in this chapter; it is kept because striking it is a modeling judgment rather than hygiene. Identity here is the message being addressed plus the list, and RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier for this — §4.4's ID clause at line 3336 is a trace-header clause on a delivered message, not a key on an originating system's expansion.
