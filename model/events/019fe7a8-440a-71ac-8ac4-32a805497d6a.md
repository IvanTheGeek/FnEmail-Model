## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. The MUST NOT at 1524-1526 governs TWO verbs - 'inspect' and 'act upon' - and the inherited set captured only the acting-upon half, as MessageDataModifiedByRelay. Inspection alone is separately forbidden, and it is the half that content filters, virus scanners and DKIM verifiers perform every day, so the violated arm is the common case in practice. Doubtful for a specific reason worth keeping: a pure read that changes nothing is exactly what the 'guest viewed calendar' test sends to set_aside - but here the read is itself prohibited, so the fact THAT it happened is a conformance state change even though the read yields nothing. Recorded, not resolved. HOTSPOT on the role asymmetry: 3.7.1 lines 1549-1552 expressly permit a GATEWAY to inspect the body 'in spite of the prohibitions in Section 6.4', so the identical act is conforming or not depending on role, which makes the actor part of the event's identity. Rejected names: RelayReadMessageBody, ContentInspectedInViolation. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck relay_host and observed_at. inspected_part survives with a real source: the prohibition names two parts, 'the header section or body of the message data' (1524-1525), so which one was read is an RFC-sourced distinction.
