## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — named as an uncarried sibling in the inherited DeliveryAttemptFailedForAddress notes. Strength: MAY, so this is the exercise of a permission rather than a violation. Distinct from CandidateAddressesExhausted and must not be merged with it: there, the list ran out; here, candidates remain and are abandoned by policy — which is exactly why untried_addresses is the essential payload and is what a later diagnosis would need. If the configured limit is 1, this event and DeliveryAbandonedAfterSingleAddress fire together, and only the second is a SHOULD violation. Precondition: AlternateAddressLimitConfigured. Rejected alternative names: AlternateAddressBudgetExhausted (collides with the genuine exhaustion event), DeliveryStoppedAtConfiguredLimit. Which project screen would have killed it: the inbound-only scope. ⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck message_id (example \<f2C8D14@foo.com>) and limit. On message_id: RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier — §4.4's ID clause is optional and written by the receiving host into its trace field (3329, 3336-3337), §6.4 line 4131 shows a message may carry no message-id at all, and f2C8D14 is this project's own minted value, absent from the RFC — and no queue_id was substituted, because the RFC defines no queue entry and no key for one; identity is the queue entry itself. limit struck as a copy of configuration state readable from AlternateAddressLimitConfigured, which is the read-model join the method's no-derived-values rule points at; a later step may restore it if the applicable ceiling turns out to need pinning at the moment of the stop. What remains is the one fact the inherited notes already named essential, and the event now carries a fact with no identity key at all — which is the honest shape of what RFC 5321 provides here.
