## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. The violated arm of a MUST NOT (578-579), and Appendix D.4 line 5111 supplies a real instance: it advertises S: 250-VRFY as an EHLO keyword although VRFY is a BASE SMTP command, not a registered service extension — one of two defects the 7.x span notes find in the RFC's own scenarios, and the rarest kind of real data available under rule 8, a violation instantiated by the specification itself. A competing reading is recorded rather than resolved: 3.5.2 lines 1364-1366 say EXPN MUST be listed as a service extension in an EHLO response and 1366-1369 that VRFY MAY be listed as a convenience, which arguably licenses 250-VRFY; if so the RFC contradicts its own 2.2.2 MUST NOT rather than merely violating it in an example. Either way a hotspot. Strength: two MUSTs and a MUST NOT in six lines (574-579), the densest normative passage in this chapter. Rejected names: NonconformingKeywordAdvertised, UnregisteredExtensionAnnounced, EhloKeywordOfferedWithoutRegistration. Screens that would have killed it: the HELO-only charter, the consumer test, the 'one event per command' target. FIELD HYGIENE: struck offering_host and receiving_client as actor and channel restatements — a keyword is offered in an EHLO response, and while the session lives the channel is its identity, RFC 5321 supplying no session key to fabricate one from. The single survivor is the whole content of the violation and it is the RFC's own S: 250-VRFY at D.4 line 5111.
