## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP; it existed in the raw set only as a name inside MailRejectedForPolicyReason's notes, never carried. This is the event that populates the 'known or identifiable sources' list, and it is the answer to how a server stops being an open relay — the single most consequential piece of configuration in the whole spec, to which §7.9 gives one sentence. Deliberately separate from RelayPolicyConfigured (which destinations may be relayed TO) and RelayDomainAuthorized (which domains VRFY may vouch for): this one is about who may ask. Rule 8 makes the difference visible — 192.0.2.10 authorized and 203.0.113.20 not, with the same RCPT TO:\<Smith@bar.com> succeeding from one and refused from the other. Opposite arm: RelaySourceRevoked. Screens: inbound-only scope keeps it, the altitude tiers file it Infrastructure, G1 finds no reader. FIELD HYGIENE: struck identification_basis ('known source'). It restated §7.9's phrase 'known or identifiable sources' without changing anything: the authorization is the same act whichever half of that phrase applies, and the RFC draws no consequence from the distinction, so the field was padding rather than one of the one-or-two meaningful facts. Both survivors are walk-scene values, and the walk above is what makes the event checkable. No session, transaction or connection key was present; identity is the server plus the authorized source address, which is the only handle the RFC's own sentence offers.

COLLISION RESOLVED — merged from 2 independently brainstormed records. Same section, same act, same actor, same stream root. The records differ only in one field's presence and in which walk-scene address they used — and the walk scene decides that: 192.0.2.10 is our server, not a client.

From the duplicate in 8. Relaying: RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. Sweep #302's notes named it ('plus the per-source RelaySourceAuthorized that populates the known list') without a slot. Kept SEPARATE from RelayAccessRestrictedToKnownSources on the principle that a set with a current value is built by events: switching filtering on is one fact, admitting one client to the list is another, and they happen at different times and by different hands. Strength: none - the RFC names 'known or identifiable sources' and never says how a source becomes known, a real gap and a HOTSPOT: the whole anti-open-relay posture rests on a list the spec does not specify the construction of. Siblings without slots: RelaySourceRevoked, RelaySourceIdentifiedByAuthentication (the non-IP basis 4.1.4 allows). Rejected names: KnownSourceAdded, RelayClientWhitelisted (rejected also for its terminology), TrustedRelayClientRegistered. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck server_host and authorized_at. The surviving value was CORRECTED: the sweep used 192.0.2.10, which in the walk scene is our own server's address, not a client's - a source authorized to relay is a client, and the walk scene's client is bar.com at 203.0.113.20. Same class of error as the next-hop IP in NextHopChannelEstablished: a walk-scene value applied to the wrong role.
