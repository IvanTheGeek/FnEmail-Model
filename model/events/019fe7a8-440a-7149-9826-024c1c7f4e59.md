## Notes

SPLIT OUT deliberately from the send, on the strength of three raw candidates that each flagged the split without taking it: UndeliverableMailNotificationSent (§3.6.3) noted "a reader who wants UndeliverableMailNotificationConstructed separately should split on lines 1496-1497"; UndeliverableMailNotificationSent (§4.4) noted "'compose and send' is one clause but two state changes"; NonDeliveryNotificationSent (§6.1) noted "'formulate' and 'mail' are arguably two events". The chapter roster itself lists "Bounce composed" as its own item, so the split is taken here. A composed notification that never gets sent is a real and common failure, which is the substantive reason. Rejected alternative names: UndeliverableMailNotificationConstructed, NotificationMessageComposed, BounceComposed, DeliveryFailureReportGenerated. Strength stack, none of which may be flattened: constructing is MUST (1496-1497, 3281-3283, 3970-3971); the grouping choice is a MUST over a disjunction — one consolidated notification OR one per failed recipient, with no third option (3285-3287); the preference for the consolidated form is SHOULD, and its stated reason is "For economy of processing by the sender" (3287-3288); using a standard DSN format is SHOULD further softened by "if possible" (1499-1501), so a non-standard bounce is conformant. The violated arm of that last SHOULD — NonStandardBounceFormatUsed — is carried as a value of report_format rather than minted as an event, because a format choice is a property of the thing composed, not a separate state change; a facilitator who disagrees should split it. grouping is likewise carried as a field rather than as the sibling pair NotificationsConsolidated / NotificationsSplitPerFailedRecipient, which is the alternative and would be right if the two shapes ever diverge in consequence. Which screen would have killed it: the consumer test (G1) — nothing in the twelve-slice model consumes a composed bounce — and the inbound-only scope.

=== FIELD HYGIENE PASS === Struck two of five fields, both fabricated identity keys. (1) notification_ref, example f2C8D14-ndn1 — doubly invented: a minted suffix on a minted queue id, and neither half is in RFC 5321. The spec gives a notification no identifier of its own. (2) subject_message_ref (f2C8D14) — same finding; §4.4's optional ID clause (rfc5321.txt:3329, 3336) is a Received:-line value local to the receiving host and cannot name a message to a different host. WHAT CARRIES IDENTITY HERE INSTEAD: the notification is identified by its recipient — the failed message's reverse-path, §6.1 3973-3974 — and by the null reverse-path it will itself carry (§6.1 3972-3973); the subject message is identified by the failed forward-paths the notification must list (§4.4 3285-3287). All three surviving fields record a choice the RFC actually poses rather than a computed value: the failed set the notification must name, the MUST-over-a-disjunction at 3285-3287, and the SHOULD-if-possible format at 1499-1501.
