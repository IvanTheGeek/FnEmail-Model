# `MessageSubmittedForDistribution`

## Notes

Carried from #67. The HANDOVER is the state change: before it the client owns the message, after it the submission server does, and the client - which by hypothesis cannot queue (1479-1481) - is free to forget it. That transfer of responsibility is what makes this an event rather than a message copy, and it is why the submission server is a relay for the purposes of this chapter: everything 3.6.2 and 3.6.3 say about a relay's obligations attaches to it from this moment. Strength: none; the spec disclaims the whole area. Sibling: MessageSubmissionRejected, the refusal arm, which this section does not describe because it does not describe submission at all - a gap, not an omission on our part. Related in other chapters: MessageSubmittedToSubmissionServer (#52) and MessageIntroducedIntoTransportEnvironment (#51) are the same handover seen from the origination chapter; rejected alternative names accordingly. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck submitted_at (event time restated) and reverse_path. The handover's two facts are who now owes delivery and what is owed. Unlike MailRelayedOnward, where the reverse-path stays because the RFC makes its being unchanged the point (4.1.1.3 lines 1943 and 1949), nothing in 3.6.3 makes the reverse-path a stated fact of a submission, so keeping it here would have been padding by analogy.
