## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. Candidate 284's notes named it explicitly — "makes RepairDocumentedInTraceHeader a sibling of this and the next two events" — and no row was ever created; the cap took it. It is the event that makes the other three auditable: without it a repaired message is indistinguishable from a well-formed one, and 4137-4139 has just said the repair may be wrong. Strength: SHOULD, and "In all cases", which is unusually broad — it covers every change a server makes, not only the three bulleted ones. Two destinations, and the RFC allows either or both: a trace header field, or a header field comment. Note the tension with §3.7.2, quoted in MailAcceptedDespiteMalformedTraceHeader: trace header fields are where this documentation goes, and receiving systems MUST NOT reject on their format — so the audit trail is deliberately unvalidated, which is a design choice worth recording rather than a gap. Screens: the inbound-only scope and the HELO-only charter.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS (chapter 14): message_id struck for the same reason as on MessageRepairRefusedByRelay — RFC 5321 has no message key, section 4.4's ID clause at line 3336 is an optional trace-header clause that may hold a queue id rather than a msg-id, and one of the very repairs being documented may be the addition of the message-id itself. Identity here is the trace header field this hop prepends, which section 4.4 defines at line 3311. documented_action and documented_in KEPT, both resting on lines 4152-4154, which name trace header fields and header field comments as the two permitted destinations; documented_action's host is corrected from mail.foo.com to foo.com, per Appendix D lines 4881 and 5064.
