## Notes

MERGE, AND A DELIBERATE SPLIT. Three inherited candidates name this state change from three spans: ListMembershipChanged{change: added\|removed} (§3.9), MailingListMemberAdded (§3.5.1), MailingListMemberAdded (§4.1.1.7). Merged, then split into two past-tense events instead of one carrying an added/removed enum — the enum makes the name a category rather than a state change, and the two have different consequences (an addition can only widen a later expansion, a removal can only narrow it). The single-event form ListMembershipChanged is the rejected alternative and is recorded here so the choice can be reversed. Other rejected names: MailingListMemberAdded, SubscriberAdded, AddressAddedToMailingList. The RFC never says how addresses get onto a list — deliberately, since subscription mechanics are outside SMTP — so this is a precondition nobody wrote down, reachable only because EXPN can read it back (§4.1.1.7 lines 2137-2139) and because §3.9's "the addresses on the list" presupposes it. member_full_name travels with the member rather than the list because §3.5.1 line 1273 makes the full name part of what EXPN MAY return. Real values are the RFC's own: Example-People and its three members at three different organizations (§3.5.1 lines 1315-1318). This is where SMTP meets a subscription context RFC 5321 does not describe at all — a translation boundary of the kind H3 resolved for the Directory, and worth naming as such rather than modeling in place. Screens: all of them — altitude tiers, inbound-only scope, responsibility-boundary cutoff, twelve-slice model. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: added_at struck — 1 field. Two reasons. Every event has a time of occurrence, and carrying it as a payload field is padding by the method's own rule; and the value instantiated was Tue, 19 May 1998 09:14:07 -0700, which is the walk scene's message-acceptance time (docs/paths/helo-direct-single-recipient.md line 39, and MessageAccepted.received_at in the v2 walk), transplanted onto a membership change it has nothing whatever to do with. RFC 5321's own 1998 dates are Thu, 21 May 1998 (lines 5014, 5064, 5066), so the value was not even the spec's. The three surviving fields are all the RFC's own data from the EXPN example at 1315-1318, which makes this one of the few records in the chapter whose payload is fully sourced. Note the identity: the List stream is keyed by the pseudo-mailbox, and RFC 5321 supplies no membership or subscription identifier — a real absence, consistent with subscription mechanics sitting outside the spec.
