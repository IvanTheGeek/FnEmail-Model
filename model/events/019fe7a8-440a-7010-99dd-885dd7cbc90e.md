## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — the violated arm of the randomization MUST. Kept as its own event rather than folded into a notes line because its consequence is unlike every other violation in this chapter: mail is delivered, correctly, and only the load distribution the MUST exists to protect is lost, so NOTHING on the wire distinguishes conformance from violation in a single session. It is visible only in aggregate, across senders, over time. That also makes it hard to call a violation at all: 3886-3887's 'no clear reason to favor one' means a client that believes it has such a reason is conforming, and no criteria are given — 🟥 inherited from the honored arm. Opposite event: EqualPreferenceExchangersRandomized. Rejected alternative names: ExchangersTriedInDnsOrder, LoadConcentratedOnOneExchanger. Which project screen would have killed it: the consumer test (G1), since nothing in the model observes load, and arguably the altitude tiers. ⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS: nothing struck; no fabricated identity key was present. order_used is an observation of what the resolver returned and the sender then used, not a computation, so it survives the derived-value test the way drawn_order does on the honored arm. preference is kept as the identity of the tie, on the same reasoning as the honored arm, and its example is illustrative because RFC 5321 supplies no preference numbers anywhere.
