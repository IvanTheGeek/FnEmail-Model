## Notes

Inherited from raw candidate ReturnAddressDeterminedInvalid, kept intact. SHOULD, and read carefully: the SHOULD governs the POLICY'S APPLICATION, not the determination itself — the determination is unregulated, and only the acting on it is constrained. This is a status-shaped event of exactly the kind the project's condemned status/phase register would reject, and the brief's corpus check says status-shaped events are legitimate, so it stands and the screen is named rather than obeyed. The RFC undercuts its own evidence in the same sentence: the determination leans on VRFY, which it says the putative sending system may not "fully and accurately support" (4051-4052) — so certainty travels as a field rather than being assumed, and evidence records what the near-certainty actually rested on. Separated by the re-sweep into three distinct events where the raw set had one: the determination (here), the drop that may follow (MessageDroppedForInvalidReturnAddress), and the standing policy that authorizes it (InvalidReturnAddressDropPolicyAdopted). The RFC keeps all three apart in one sentence and the model should too. Rejected alternative names: InvalidReturnAddressFound, ReversePathJudgedInvalid, SenderAddressDeterminedBogus. Which screens would have killed it: the condemned status/phase register, the capture/decision two-kinds lens, and the altitude tiers.
