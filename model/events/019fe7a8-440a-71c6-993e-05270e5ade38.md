## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP from §4.3.2 lines 3061-3063, and it absorbs candidate 25 (VerificationRefusedAsUnimplemented), whose §3.5.3 citation at "1378-1381" is corrected here: the quoted sentence begins at 1380, not 1378. The required subset is the nine commands listed at §4.5.1 lines 3384-3392 — EHLO, HELO, MAIL, RCPT, DATA, RSET, NOOP, QUIT, VRFY — which is why VRFY is the real example: it is the only one of the nine that implementations routinely refuse. Candidate 25 found a genuine ambiguity in the source and it is preserved verbatim rather than resolved: 1375-1377 prescribes 502 or 500 for the syntax-only case and 1380-1381 calls returning 500 or 502 for VRFY not full compliance. Read together the spec prescribes a reply and then calls sending it non-conforming; the presumable resolution is that a compliant server never reaches the syntax-only case because it actually verifies, but the RFC does not say so, and rule 3 forbids inventing the bridge. Rejected alternative names: VerificationRefusedAsUnimplemented (too narrow — the violation covers all nine), MinimumImplementationBreached. Distinct from CommandRejectedAsUnimplemented: 502 for an optional command is legal, 500 or 502 for a required one is not. Screens: the condemned status register would read the compliance state as status.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS (chapter 14): session_id struck — RFC 5321 names no session, transaction or connection identifier, and f2C8D14 is nowhere in the RFC; identity here is position in the session. reply_code struck as the decision rendered and as a constant implied by the event's meaning. command_verb KEPT and sourced to the required-command list itself at line 3392: the verb is what makes the refusal a violation, since the identical reply to a verb outside the nine is legal, so this is the one field the event cannot lose.
