## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. §7.3 names an actor that appears nowhere else in the chapter and nowhere in the inherited set: the party on the other end of EXPN, acting adversarially. MailingListMembershipDisclosedViaExpn records the server's act; this records the requester's, and they are not the same event — same wire exchange, two parties, two different things that became true. Keeping both is the instructions' 'a relay's send is not an originator's' applied to a disclosure. Strength: none — §7.3 describes an observed trend in flat prose, with no 2119 keyword anywhere in the paragraph. Marked doubtful for exactly that reason, and because whether a request is harvesting or diagnosis is not observable at the moment it happens; the RFC nonetheless treats the distinction as real enough to justify disabling the command. Rejected alternative names: ListHarvestedBySpammer (rejected — 'spammer' is the RFC's word in scare quotes at 4254, not a domain role), AddressSourceCollected, InappropriateListUseDetected (rejected — detection is a different event with a different actor). Which screen would have killed it: the inbound-only scope, the responsibility-boundary cutoff, the consumer test, and the four altitude tiers at once. Counterweight the RFC itself supplies at 4265-4273: VRFY and EXPN remain useful for authenticated users and internal audits, and implementations SHOULD still provide support for EXPN. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: nothing struck. No reply code and no invented identity key. What the sourcing pass exposes is worth recording: every value here is either the RFC's own EXPN example or the walk scene's client address, and the actor — the harvester — has no identity the protocol carries at all beyond that address. §7.3 names the party and gives it nothing, which is why requesting_client is the only handle this event has, and it is exactly the kind of absence a fabricated session_id would have hidden. Left doubtful, for the reasons already recorded.
