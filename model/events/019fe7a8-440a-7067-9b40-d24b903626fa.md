## Notes

Carried from #85, with #314 (NonDeliveryNoticeReturned, Appendix C) noted as the same state change under a rejected name; other rejected names: BounceMessageGenerated, UndeliverableMailReported. THREE strengths are stacked here and must not be flattened. (1) Constructing and sending is MUST, 1496-1499. (2) Using a standard DSN format is SHOULD, softened by 'if possible', 1499-1501, so a non-standard bounce is conformant. (3) Who the notification comes from is a LOWERCASE 'must' at 1503, not the RFC 2119 keyword - an editorial slip or a deliberate non-normative statement, and exactly what rule 3 exists to catch; verified by opening the file. A candidate NonDeliveryReportFormatApplied was folded into report_format rather than padding the count. Opposite: UndeliverableMailSilentlyDiscarded. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck sent_at (event time restated) and notifying_host. The loss is recorded honestly: 1503-1505 DOES make the from-host a stated fact and allows two different hosts, but for an event recorded by that host it is the recorder's own identity; if the model ever records notifications observed from outside, the field returns with foo.com (5001) as its value. report_format kept - it carries a separate normative statement about this event.

COLLISION RESOLVED — kept separate and renamed. Different sections, different actors, different normative content: the bias applies squarely. One rests on 4.4 lines 3275-3294 and 6.1 lines 3970-3975, where the sender is the system that accepted responsibility at the 250; the other rests on 3.6.3 lines 1494-1505, where the sender is a relay that accepted the task of relaying. Neither cites the other's section. The chapter 5 record already carried an explicit, reasoned instruction not to merge — a relay's send is not an originator's — and the RFC backs the distinction with content that exists on only one side: the DSN-format SHOULD and the lowercase from-host "must" in 3.6.3, the grouping MUST and the partial-delivery setting in 4.4. Note the deliberate asymmetry with DeliveryFailedAfterAcceptance above, which IS merged: 3.6.3 lines 1503-1505 unify the DETERMINER across roles in one sentence ("the host that first determines that delivery cannot be accomplished"), so the determination is one datable fact, while the SENDING obligations are stated twice, for two roles, with different requirements. If a later pass finds the two send-side obligation sets genuinely identical, these two can be collapsed then — a split loses nothing now, where a wrong merge would bury one record's research.

The resolver's full account: Two events, renamed. (1) UndeliverableMailNotificationSentByAcceptingServer — chapter 5 (Responsibility Handoff and Final Delivery), actor a notifying SMTP server now acting as an originating system, stream root Message, refs 4.4 lines 3281-3283, 3285-3288 and 3291-3294, 6.1 lines 3972-3973 and 3973-3975, 4.4 lines 3258-3260; fields notification_recipient, failed_recipients (List), envelope_sender (MAIL FROM:\<>, rfc5321.txt:1522), grouping. (2) UndeliverableMailNotificationSentByRelay — chapter 8 (Relaying), actor a relay SMTP server or the first host to determine non-delivery, stream root Notification Message, refs 3.6.3 lines 1496-1499, 1499-1501 and 1503-1505; fields sent_to (\<JQP@bar.com>, rfc5321.txt:5058), undelivered_forward_path (\<Jones@XYZ.COM>, rfc5321.txt:5060), report_format (RFC 3464, rfc5321.txt:1500). HOW THEY DIFFER: the first is sent by a system that took custody of the message at the 250 and then could not deliver it, and it carries the grouping obligation peculiar to that position — a single notification listing all failed recipients or separate ones per recipient MUST be sent, with the consolidated form SHOULD-preferred "For economy of processing by the sender" — which only arises where several recipients were accepted in one transaction and only some failed (4.4 lines 3275-3283). The second is sent by a relay that accepted the TASK OF RELAYING and later found the destination incorrect, and it carries two normative statements the first does not: the SHOULD to use a standard non-delivery report format, softened by "if possible" so a non-standard bounce is still conformant (1499-1501), and a LOWERCASE "must" at 1503 on who the notification comes from — not the RFC 2119 keyword, an editorial slip or a deliberate non-normative statement, verified by opening the file, and exactly the kind of thing rule 3 exists to catch. Each record's notes travel with its own survivor, and both must cross-reference the other. Carried into (1): the merge of five inherited candidates naming one act from five places, with rejected names NonDeliveryNotificationSent, NonDeliveryNotificationReturned, SubsequentFailureNotificationReturned, DeliveryFailureReportGenerated, BounceSent, MessageBounced, and the observation that 6.2 lines 4019-4020 call the same act "bounced" — one act, two vocabularies inside one document; the SIX MUSTs that stack and must not be flattened (compose and send; one grouping form or the other; use the MAIL command even for the obsolete SEND/SOML/SAML; use a null return path; recipient equals the envelope return path; the reverse-path is the target of any delivery error mail); the two modeling choices recorded so nobody re-derives them — "compose and send" is one clause but two state changes, so NotificationMessageComposed / NotificationMessageSent is a defensible sibling pair since a composed notification that never sends is a real and common failure, and grouping is carried as a field rather than as sibling events NotificationsConsolidated / NotificationsSplitPerFailedRecipient, which becomes the right call if the two shapes ever diverge in consequence; envelope_sender is written in the literal wire form MAIL FROM:\<> because the brackets are path syntax and MAIL FROM: alone is a different thing, and its violated arm has its own row as NotificationSentWithNonNullReturnPath; original_message_id struck as fabricated, with the hotspot it exposes preserved — what links this notification to the original is ONLY the address it goes to and the failed recipients it lists, and that missing correlation is why bounce matching is a practical problem; sent_at struck as the event's own occurrence time.
