# `SourceRouteStripped`

## Notes

MERGE of three raw candidates: SourceRouteStripped (§4.1 sweep), SourceRouteIgnored (§3.1-3.3 sweep) and SourceRouteStripped (§3.6-3.7 sweep). Rejected alternative names: SourceRouteIgnored, RouteInformationDiscarded, AtDomainListStripped. STRENGTHS MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED — four different ones ride on this single act across the four citations: MUST recognize / MUST be prepared, SHOULD strip, MAY ignore, and at 1430-1433 a MAY and a SHOULD on the same sentence (permission to ignore plus a recommendation to exercise the permission). A careless citation reports only one half; read as one requirement it is effectively SHOULD, read literally the MAY grants the capability and the SHOULD ranks it. This is a payload-MUTATION event: the forward-path the server acts on is not the forward-path it was given, and the discarded hops vanish from the record unless stripped_at_domains keeps them. Example values are the RFC's own from 1943-1950. Siblings: SourceRoutedAddressRefused (the other arm — refuse instead of strip), SourceRouteNamesCopiedIntoReversePath (the violated arm of the MUST NOT at 1930-1931), and a predicted consequence the RFC itself names at 1433-1437, delivery failing after the route is stripped, which belongs to the delivery chapter. Screens: the two-kinds lens, and the consumer test — nothing in a single-recipient direct-delivery path consumes a stripped route.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck three fields. transaction_id (f2C8D14-t1) goes because RFC 5321 supplies no transaction identifier and f2C8D14 appears nowhere in the document — it is this project's own minted queue-id example from commit 6acfc68; identity here is the received forward-path, on the transaction open on the channel. retained_forward_path and stripped_at_domains struck as COMPUTED: the RFC supplies the derivation rule itself, twice — 'utilize the domain name associated with the mailbox as if the source route had not been provided' (1926-1928) and 'simply send to the final destination specified as the last element in the route' (1430-1433) — and then performs the derivation in its own worked example, turning RCPT TO:\<@hosta.int,@jkl.org:userc@d.bar.org> at 1944 into RCPT TO:\<userc@d.bar.org> at 1950. A value the source computes by a stated rule from another field on the same event belongs in a read model. ⚠️ The note above argues that 'the discarded hops vanish from the record unless stripped_at_domains keeps them'; they do not — received_forward_path keeps every hop, and the retained path and the stripped list are both recoverable from it. The mutation the note calls the point of the event is still visible: the event says a source route was received and stripped, and what it was stripped to follows by the RFC's own rule.

COLLISION RESOLVED — merged from 2 independently brainstormed records. Both are themselves merges of three raw candidates each, over overlapping sections, and they land on the same act, actor, stream root, field and RFC example value. The reference sets are complementary rather than conflicting.

From the duplicate in 8. Relaying: Merged from #71 (3.6.1), #144 (3.3) and #170 (4.1.1.3); rejected names: SourceRouteIgnored, RouteInformationDiscarded, AtDomainListRemoved. STRENGTH IS UNUSUAL AND WORTH RECORDING PRECISELY: one sentence at 1430-1433 carries MAY and SHOULD on the same act - permission to ignore, plus a recommendation to exercise that permission. Read as a single requirement it is effectively SHOULD; read literally the MAY grants the capability and the SHOULD ranks it. Across sections the strengths multiply: MUST recognize / MUST BE accepted (4.1.2 writes 'MUST BE' with the space at 2266-2268, copied verbatim per rule 2), SHOULD strip, SHOULD NOT generate. A second MUST NOT rides along at 1930-1931 - source-route names MUST NOT be copied into the reverse-path. Related but NOT merged: OwnDomainRemovedFromForwardPath (#312, Appendix C lines 4823-4826). Opposite: SourceRouteHonored. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck ignored_hops and final_destination, correcting the inherited reasoning rather than applying it quietly. That note defended ignored_hops because 'the discarded hops vanish from the record unless this event keeps them' - they do not: the offered path is kept whole, and both the hops and the final destination are readings of it under the 4.1.2 grammar (2264-2269).
