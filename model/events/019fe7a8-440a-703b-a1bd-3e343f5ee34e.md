# `ReceivedLinePrependedByGateway`

A gateway put its own Received: line at the front of the header section as the message crossed into or out of the Internet environment, leaving every existing line untouched.

## Where RFC 5321 says so

| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| §3.7.2 | 1577-1579 | *"When forwarding a message into or out of the Internet environment, a gateway MUST prepend a Received: line, but it MUST NOT alter in any way a Received: line that is already in the header section."* |
| §2.3.10 | 816-818 | *"A "gateway" SMTP system (usually referred to just as a "gateway") receives mail from a client system in one transport environment and transmits it to a server system in another transport environment."* |
| §3.7.2 | 1583-1585 | *"the most important use of Received: lines is for debugging mail faults, and this debugging can be severely hampered by well-meaning gateways that try to "fix" a Received: line."* |

## Notes

DELIBERATELY NOT MERGED with TraceLinePrepended, per the brief's warning against merging things that merely look alike: §3.7.2 is a separate MUST addressed to a separate actor at a boundary crossing, and the direction field is a fact a relay's stamp has no analogue for. A gateway is also expressly allowed transformations 'that are not permitted to SMTP relay systems' (§2.3.10 820-822), so the same act sits inside different permissions. Two MUSTs in one sentence pointing opposite ways: MUST prepend, MUST NOT alter what is there — the second arm is carried by ReceivedLineChainAltered, whose §3.7.2 citations are unioned in there. Note the vocabulary difference the inherited record spotted and I confirmed: §3.7.2 says 'prepend' where §3.6.3's relay text (1524-1526) says only 'add', so a reader who cites §3.6.3 alone under-states the position obligation and must fetch it from §4.4 3180-3182. Companion event in the same subsection: ViaClauseRecorded. Neighboring chapter: the gatewaying chapter owns §3.7.1, §3.7.3, §3.7.4 and §3.7.5; only §3.7.2 is mine.
