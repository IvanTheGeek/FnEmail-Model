## Notes

THREE candidates merged: BufferCleared (§2.3.6 sweep), TransactionBuffersReset (§3.3 sweep) and TransactionBuffersCleared (§4.1.1 sweep). Rejected alternative names: TransactionBuffersReset, TransactionBuffersCleared, SessionReset, StateTablesCleared. clearing_command is a field rather than four sibling events because the identical clearing happens on MAIL, on completed DATA, on RSET, and on an accepted mid-session greeting — four sites, four MUSTs. Recording the discarded values matters: a model that records only 'reset happened' cannot answer what was thrown away, which is rule 8's point exactly — \<Smith@bar.com> teaches what a placeholder cannot. Recorded and NOT carried as its own event, cut for the cap: §4.1.1.5 line 2084 makes a '250 OK' reply to an argument-free RSET a MUST, which is the reset's two-party confirmation and the exact counterpart of SessionInitialStateConfirmed; a facilitator wanting symmetry should add ResetAcknowledged. Strength oddity worth keeping: the §3.3 sentence carries no 2119 keyword at all — it merely 'tells the SMTP-receiver ... to reset' — which is stronger than a SHOULD in practice and weaker in letter. Honest objection kept: §4.1.1 frames the buffers as 'The model for this is', an explanatory device, so this may be an artifact of the RFC's own exposition rather than domain state. Screens: all of them — capture/decision lens, consumer test, altitude tiers, condemned status register. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck 1 field, the lightest strike in the chapter, because the three survivors are all sourced to the RFC's own D.1 walk. session_id struck — no such key in the spec; identity here is the buffer contents themselves, which is why the discarded paths matter and why this event, not any identifier, is what names the transaction SessionReinitialized throws away. All three surviving examples come from the RFC's own worked example at lines 4893 and 4895, and clearing_command from the §4.1.1.5 heading at line 2079.
