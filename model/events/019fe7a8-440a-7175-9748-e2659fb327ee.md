## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP as the violated and unsupported arms of the SHOULD/MAY pair at 2161-2162, which the §4.1 sweep noted and then discarded as 'too thin to earn a slot'. Two distinct causes share this event and a facilitator may split them: 502 means the whole command is unimplemented, contradicting the SHOULD at 2161; 504 means the argument form is unimplemented, which is fully permitted because argument support is only a MAY. So one of the two arms is a spec deviation and the other is not, and the reply code is the only thing that distinguishes them. HELP is absent from the §4.5.1 minimum command list at lines 3384-3392, unlike NOOP and VRFY, which is why a 502 here is a weaker deviation than a 502 to VRFY. Rejected alternative names: HelpUnsupported, HelpArgumentRejected (that is one arm only), HelpCommandNotImplemented. Sibling: HelpInformationSent. Which screen would have killed it: the consumer test, 'one event per command', the twelve-slice model, and the whole HELP subject under the HELO-only charter's neighbor screens. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck two fields, and THIS IS THE EVENT WHERE THE COST OF THE REPLY-CODE RULE IS HIGHEST IN THIS CHAPTER — recorded, not hidden. session_id (f2C8D14) went as a fabricated identity key; RFC 5321 supplies none, and identity here is the channel plus the command's position in the sequence. reply_code (504) went as a wire rendering — but the note above already says in as many words that the reply code is the only thing distinguishing this event's two causes, so with it gone the record can no longer tell a 502 (whole command unimplemented, a deviation from the SHOULD at 2161) from a 504 (argument form unimplemented, fully permitted by the MAY at 2161-2162). One of those arms is a spec deviation and the other is not. That is a splitting decision for a later step, and both arms are named here and in the rfc_refs so the step can make it.
