## Where RFC 5321 says so

| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP from §4.5.4, immediately above the §4.5.4.1 anchor; no inherited candidate carried it. Two MUSTs, one of them a MUST NOT reinforced by 'under any circumstances' (3682-3683) — that phrasing appears nowhere else in the retry sections, and its violated arm is the classic mail loop: two systems answering each other's bounces forever. The RFC treats the queuing strategy as a thing adopted once and thereafter binding, which makes it configuration rather than behavior. Related loop protections live in §6.1-6.3 and belong to other chapters; cross-referenced rather than duplicated. If the roster prefers, the two MUSTs split into ErrorLoopSuppressionConfigured and QueueTimeoutPolicyAdopted — kept as one because the RFC states them as two properties of one adopted strategy. FIELD HYGIENE: struck per_command_timeouts and error_on_error_suppressed. Both were the MUSTs at 3681-3683 restated as Booleans that are true in every conforming instance, so they recorded the requirement rather than a fact about this adoption; if the violated arms are ever wanted they are separate events, as the split named above already anticipates. host survives alone, which is the method's explicitly sanctioned 'just the identity key' case. No session, transaction or connection key was present, and note the related absence: a mail queue entry is described at 3692-3693 as message plus envelope information, with no identifier of its own anywhere in RFC 5321 — the §4.4 ID clause is the closest the spec comes, and it is optional and belongs to the trace header rather than to the queue.
