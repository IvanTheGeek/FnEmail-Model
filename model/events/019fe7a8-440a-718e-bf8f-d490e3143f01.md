## Where RFC 5321 says so

| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| §7.9 | 4357-4359 | *"It is a well-established principle that an SMTP server may refuse to accept mail for any operational or technical reason that makes sense to the site providing the server."* |
| §7.9 | 4359-4365 | *"However, cooperation among sites and installations makes the Internet possible. If sites take excessive advantage of the right to reject traffic, the ubiquity of email availability (one of the strengths of the Internet) will be threatened; considerable care should be taken and balance maintained if a site decides to be selective about the traffic it will accept and process."* |

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP as the standing-configuration arm of §7.9; the raw set carried only the runtime arm, MailRejectedForPolicyReason, which is ceded to the session/transaction chapters and cross-referenced here. The permission at 4357-4359 is unconditional and lowercase 'may'; the restraint at 4363 is a lowercase 'should' with no 2119 force at all — the RFC's strongest words here are its weakest keywords, which is worth recording because a reader skimming for MUSTs finds nothing and concludes there is no rule. The SHOULD at 4380 attaches to the reply CODE, not to the refusal. Three landing points named explicitly at 4381 — EHLO (or HELO), MAIL, RCPT — and a 550 at EHLO ends the session before any transaction exists, which the one-event-per-command target has no shape for. Kept distinct from RelayPolicyConfigured: relaying is one ground among many, and §7.9's first paragraph is broader than its second. FIELD HYGIENE: struck policy_reply_code (550). The SHOULD at 4379-4381 attaches the code to a reply sent at runtime 'in response to EHLO (or HELO), MAIL, or RCPT', not to anything this configuration event stores, so the code is the rendering of a later decision and belongs to the runtime arm named above. refusal_ground survives with its value taken verbatim from line 4358; it is genuinely open by the RFC's own wording ('any operational or technical reason that makes sense to the site'), which is the fact that makes the event meaningful. No session, transaction or connection key was present; identity is the site named by its server's domain.
