## Notes

Inherited candidate 39, kept. The client-side twin of ResponsibilityForMessageAccepted, and stated in §2.1 with the same either/or shape one paragraph before the server's version — transfer or report, deliver or report. Worth putting the two sentences side by side on the wall: the whole of SMTP's reliability story is that both ends carry an obligation that can only be discharged two ways, and every event in this chapter is one of the four discharges. The report's destination is the envelope originator address, defined by its error-report role rather than by authorship, which is easy to lose when a model treats MAIL FROM as 'the sender'. ⚠️ Inherited citation dropped: candidate 39 also cited §2.3.1 lines 613-624 for the originator address; that is a definition of the mail object's envelope, not of this event, and it is not reproduced here. Screens: inbound-only scope (this is a client-side event) and the responsibility-boundary cutoff. ⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS 2026-08-09 — two fields struck. message_id: RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier. §4.4's ID clause (line 3336, `ID = CFWS "ID" FWS ( Atom / msg-id )`) is an OPTIONAL clause inside the receiver's own Received: stamp — and this event is the client's, upstream of any receiver's trace — while msg-id is RFC 5322's, not this document's. Its example f2C8D14 is this project's minted queue_id value (commit 6acfc68) and appears nowhere in the RFC. Identity here is the report's destination — the envelope originator address the report is addressed to — together with the domain that could not be reached. reported_at: struck as the event's own occurrence time.
