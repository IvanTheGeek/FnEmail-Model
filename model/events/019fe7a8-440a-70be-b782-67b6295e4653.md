## Notes

Rejected alternative names: ReversePathAllowed (FnEmail's own settled name for the equivalent step), SenderAccepted, MailFromAccepted. The RFC's own word is 'accepted', which is why it is kept here — but the project has ruled on ReversePathAllowed and a later step should reconcile the two deliberately rather than by drift. CRITICAL PAIRING: on the wire this is INDISTINGUISHABLE from ReversePathProvisionallyAccepted — both are a bare 250 — and that is exactly why the two are separate events. The server's internal state differs even though the client sees one reply, and a model keyed only on the wire cannot tell them apart. Opposite events: ReversePathRejectedPermanently, ReversePathRejectedTemporarily, TransactionCommandArgumentRejected.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck one field, reply_code (250). A reply code is the rendering of a decision on the wire, and the decision is what this event's name already carries; the method forbids computed and derived values on events. The strike also sharpens the note above rather than weakening it: the whole point of keeping this event separate from ReversePathProvisionallyAccepted is that the 250 does NOT distinguish them, so a reply_code field on both was carrying the one fact that cannot tell them apart. §3.3 1034-1035 supplies the code and is cited above, so nothing is lost. No identity key was present to strike; identity is the reverse-path being accepted, plus position in the session.
