## Notes

Carried from #73 with its doubt intact, under the rule that Step 1 keeps what it is unsure of. The sentence states an IDENTITY, not an act: nothing is computed, transformed or decided, so it may be a rule rather than an event. Kept because it is the delivery-time counterpart of something this project has already named - ReversePathDeclared, the settled name for the step 5 event - and a facilitator with that name on the wall will ask what the reverse-path is at the OTHER end; its value, if it survives, is that it closes a loop across the whole transaction with no intervening change. Strength: none; declarative. Opposite, from the legacy world this sentence retires: RelayHostPrependedToReversePath (#313, Appendix C lines 4841-4845) is the RFC 821 construction process 3.6.1 declares inapplicable - cross-referenced, not merged. Rejected names: ReversePathCarriedToDelivery, ReversePathUnchangedAtDelivery. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck resolved_at (event time restated as payload). The single survivor is the whole content of the sentence, and Appendix D.3 instantiates the claim: the same \<JQP@bar.com> in the MAIL command on the inbound leg (5008) and on the onward leg (5058). Confidence left doubtful and unchanged - the doubt is about whether an identity is an event, which stripping a timestamp neither creates nor cures.
