## Notes

Directly relevant to docs/smtp-path-vs-mailbox.md: the envelope/header distinction the whole model rests on is not universal, and here it is collapsed on purpose. Strength: NONE — "may be necessary" (line 1558) describes a situation, and X-SMTP-MAIL:/X-SMTP-RCPT: is explicitly only "A possible solution" (line 1559). Do NOT cite those field names as standard; they are an illustration the RFC itself immediately argues against, and Appendix E lines 5132-5136 calls exactly this shortcut "inadequate in important ways" — so §3.7.1 floats a technique that Appendix E condemns, which is a contradiction inside the document worth a hotspot. Consequence split out by the re-sweep into its own event, PrivateRecipientDisclosedByEnvelopeFolding; the capped sweep had folded it into this one's notes. Rejected alternative names: SmtpEnvelopeCarriedInHeaderFields, EnvelopeInformationMigratedToHeader. Which project screen would have killed it: inbound-only scope, altitude tiers.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck message_ref (example f2C8D14) — no message identifier exists in RFC 5321; what identifies this fold is the envelope it carried, which is why the two path fields stay. ⚠️ DEFECT FOUND BY SETTING example_source, and it is exactly rule 8's kind of find: the two paths were INVERTED. carried_reverse_path read \<Jones@foo.com> and carried_forward_paths read \<Smith@bar.com>, but in the walk scene and in the RFC's own Appendix D session (MAIL FROM:\<Smith@bar.com> at line 4893, RCPT TO:\<Jones@foo.com> at line 4895) Smith@bar.com is the sender and Jones@foo.com the recipient. A reverse-path example that is actually a forward path teaches the reader the opposite of the distinction this event exists to expose. Swapped, and recorded rather than quietly fixed.
