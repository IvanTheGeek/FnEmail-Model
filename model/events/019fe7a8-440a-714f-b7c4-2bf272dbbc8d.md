## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — the other arm of the §6.2 SHOULD NOT, reachable two ways and worth distinguishing from an ordinary bounce for exactly that reason. Arm one: the escape clause is satisfied and the send is conformant. Arm two: the escape clause is not satisfied and the send violates the SHOULD NOT, producing what operators call backscatter — a bounce mailed to a forged return address. The RFC gives the site no test for its own confidence, so the two arms are indistinguishable from the outside and only the sending site knows which one happened: that is why confidence_basis is a recorded fact rather than a derived flag, and why this cannot simply be folded into NonDeliveryNotificationSent with a reason. Strength: permitted only by the exception to a SHOULD NOT, against an explicitly stated default to the contrary (4061-4063). Opposite event: BounceSuppressedForHostileContent. Rejected alternative names: HostileContentBounceSent, BackscatterGenerated, RejectionNotificationSentWithConfidence. Which screens would have killed it: the inbound-only scope and the consumer test (G1); the altitude tiers would put the confidence judgment outside the protocol tier entirely.

=== FIELD HYGIENE PASS === Struck one of three fields: message_ref (f2C8D14), fabricated — RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier. WHAT CARRIES IDENTITY HERE INSTEAD: the address the bounce went to, which is the reverse-path of the rejected message. Example corrected: notification_recipient read \<Jones@foo.com>, the walk scene's recipient; a bounce goes to the originator, \<Smith@bar.com>. confidence_basis SURVIVES on the record's own argument — it is the only thing distinguishing the conformant arm from the backscatter arm — but its example is now the RFC's bare escape clause instead of an invented rationale about known correspondent domains and forged return addresses, because the specification supplies no criterion whatever. That unsourceable example is itself the signal item 5 describes, and it is left standing rather than hidden: a facilitator may reasonably cut the field and let this event carry the recipient alone.
