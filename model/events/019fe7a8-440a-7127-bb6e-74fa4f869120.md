## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP from Appendix E, outside the roster's anchors. The capped sweep named "EnvelopeInformationLostInTranslation" as "a legitimate sibling event" in MessageGatewayedFromForeignMailSystem's notes and gave it no slot; this is that card. It is the honest name for what the whole chapter is about: the RFC concedes the loss rather than specifying it away, which is rare enough to be worth a card of its own. Strength: LOWERCASE "must be written with the understanding" at line 5138 — an instruction to implementers about their expectations, NOT the RFC 2119 keyword and not a requirement on any message. That is the third lowercase near-miss in this chapter (with §2.3.10 line 823 and §3.7.5 line 1637) and the fourth if Appendix B line 4776 is counted; the pattern is that gatewaying prose slips out of normative register whenever it stops being able to specify. Kept distinct from LayeringSemanticsPreservedAcrossBoundary: that is the attempt, this is the residue, and the loss happens even when the attempt is honored ("almost inevitable"). Rejected alternative names: TranslationInformationLoss, EnvelopeFactsDroppedAtBoundary. Which project screen would have killed it: consumer test / G1, altitude tiers, inbound-only scope.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck message_ref (example f2C8D14) — RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier, and this card is the one place where that absence is the subject rather than an inconvenience: the event exists to say that facts do not survive a crossing, and a fabricated key would have implied a thread of identity that survives it. lost_information is kept as genuine payload — naming WHAT was lost is the only content the card has — but its example is corrected from "envelope reverse-path \<Jones@foo.com>" to \<Smith@bar.com>: Jones@foo.com is the walk scene's recipient, so the old value described a reverse-path that was actually a forward path. Fourth inversion of that kind in the chapter.
