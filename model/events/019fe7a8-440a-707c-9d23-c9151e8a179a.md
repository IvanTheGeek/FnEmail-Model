## Notes

Carried unmerged. The one lookup in this chapter that survives the kit's guest-viewed-calendar test, and it survives on the RFC's own wording: the information IS captured, so a durable artifact exists that outlives the reply. That is why VRFY and EXPN go to set_aside and this does not. Strengths: MAY to verify, MUST NOT to act on failure — and that MUST NOT is narrow, scoped to the matching of the parameter to its IP address only, with §7.9 explicitly left open for other refusal grounds. A future reader would otherwise re-derive that distinction from scratch, or worse, read the MUST NOT as a general prohibition on IP-based refusal. Screens: the four altitude tiers (a log record reads as Infrastructure) and the consumer test, since nothing in the protocol consumes the log. The real find is the explicit prohibition on a decision the model would otherwise be tempted to draw. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck 1 field. session_id struck — no such key in RFC 5321; identity on this event is the pair being checked, and §4.1.4 lines 2481-2482 makes that pair — the domain argument and the client's IP address — the literal subject of the verification, which is why both survive here after client_address was struck from ClientIdentityDeclared. verification_result is kept despite the ban on computed values, because §4.1.4 lines 2483-2485 says the information is captured for logging and tracing: the RFC makes the recording of the outcome the point of the act. Its example has no RFC source and is marked illustrative.
