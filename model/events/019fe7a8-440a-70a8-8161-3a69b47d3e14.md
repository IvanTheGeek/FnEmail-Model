## Notes

Harvested. The RFC states this TWICE — at the top of §3.3 and again after the DATA discussion — which is the argument for keeping it as its own fact rather than folding it into EndOfMailDataIndicated: confirmation is a distinct meaning from termination, carried by one act. The dot confirms; the 250 accepts; between them the server processes. Note the actor: the CLIENT confirms the transaction, by an act that looks like a terminator — which is why forward_paths is a List here (the confirmation covers every recipient the transaction accumulated) and why the capture/decision two-kinds lens, which would collapse confirm and accept into one moment, is the screen that kills it. Rejected alternative names: TransactionCommitted, MailTransactionCompleted. Genuine ambiguity for a later step, recorded not resolved: whether EndOfMailDataIndicated and MailTransactionConfirmed are one event with two consequences or two events on one act. The RFC's own wording ("also confirms") supports two.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck two of four. transaction_id is fabricated — RFC 5321 names no transaction identifier, so what identifies the confirmed transaction is exactly what the two surviving fields hold: the reverse-path and forward-path buffers it accumulated. That is why they are kept rather than treated as a fold of the MAIL and RCPT events — with no key in the spec, the paths are the identity, and §3.3 1116-1117 names "the stored recipients and mail" as what the confirmation releases. confirmed_at is struck as the event's own occurrence time. Both values are the walk scene's and are also the RFC's own, at rfc5321.txt:4893 and rfc5321.txt:4895 in Appendix D.

Fieldless is the right and expected shape for a commit: the event's content is that the accumulated transaction is now confirmed, and everything accumulated is already in the stream.
