## Notes

Carried from #72. Absorbs a candidate the inherited sweep also absorbed, UnresolvableDestinationNameAccepted - the precondition where a sender addressed mail to a name that is not in the DNS, counting on the route to fix it. A reader who wants the precondition separate should split and cite the same lines 1433-1436; it is merged because the RFC states practice and consequence in one breath and the failure is the only point where state actually changes. Strength: NONE - a statement of consequence, not a requirement, which is why it is easy to miss and why a keyword-hunting re-sweep would not have found it. Related client-side act: InvalidSourceRouteGenerated (the MUST NOT at 1437-1439 that this consequence motivates - 'This is one of several reasons why'). Rejected names: StrippedRouteDeliveryFailure, DestinationUnresolvableAfterStripping. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck final_destination and stripped_hops, both readings of a path already recorded upstream by SourceRouteStripped. The one survivor is the fact the sentence turns on. Its example is honestly split in the source line: the host name is the RFC's own destination at 1946, while its absence from the DNS is illustrative, because 1433-1436 describes the practice and instantiates no unresolvable name anywhere.
