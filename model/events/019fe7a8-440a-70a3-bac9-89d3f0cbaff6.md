# `HeaderValueEncodedForAsciiTransport`

An originating system represented a header value that lies outside US-ASCII using an encoding that is itself US-ASCII, so the header section could cross an unextended SMTP channel.

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP of §2.3.1 — no sweep produced anything from lines 639-643, and it is the only place in the chapter's anchors where content is TRANSFORMED before transfer rather than during it. Strength: none in this document; the algorithms are RFC 2047 and RFC 2231 and this section only points at them. Doubtful for two separate reasons a later step should keep apart: the act happens before any session exists (so it may belong to a submission chapter), and the encoding may be better modeled as a property of the header field than as an event. Kept because §2.3.1 is one of this chapter's named anchors and returning nothing from it would be a silent gap. The word "always" at 639 is the load-bearing one — it is what makes the 8BITMIME relaxation apply to the body only, and it is the same fact HighOrderBitCleared depends on. Screens: the inbound-only scope and the responsibility-boundary cutoff both kill it, since the actor is on the far side of the wire.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck one of three. transaction_id is fabricated twice over — RFC 5321 supplies no transaction identifier at all, and this act happens before any SMTP session exists, so there is not even a channel to stand in for one. That absence is the finding rather than a gap: nothing in RFC 5321 identifies a message before it is offered with MAIL. Both surviving fields are RFC-sourced: the header field name Subject at rfc5321.txt:1153, and RFC 2047 at rfc5321.txt:640, where §2.3.1 names the two algorithms. Which of the two encodings was applied is a genuine fact about the instance, not a restatement, so encoding_standard stays.
