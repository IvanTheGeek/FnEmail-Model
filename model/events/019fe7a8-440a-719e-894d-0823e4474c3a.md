# `RelayTaskAccepted`

A relay server took on the task of carrying a message onward to another system for a particular address, the same way it would accept mail for a local user.

## Notes

Merged from #74 and #317; rejected names: RelayResponsibilityAccepted, RelayingObligationAssumed. Strength: lowercase 'may accept or reject' (1450), permission not a keyword, yet this arm arms the span's only MUST. Line 1494 opens with it already past tense ('has accepted the task') - a precondition nobody wrote down, and naming it makes the notification obligation's trigger visible. Acceptance of a TASK, not of a message. Distinct from DeliveryResponsibilityAccepted (#272, 6.1 lines 3962-3964, the 250 OK after DATA): custody and the per-recipient relay obligation are different facts. Opposite: RelayTaskDeclined. Terminal outcomes: MailRelayedOnward, or DeliveryDeterminedImpossible into UndeliverableMailNotificationSent. Screens: inbound-only scope, responsibility-boundary cutoff. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck relay_host (the recording relay's own domain, ambient to it, and mx.bar.com appears nowhere in the RFC) and accepted_at (the event's own occurrence time restated as payload). No session/transaction/connection key was present to strike and none exists - grep over rfc5321.txt returns nothing - so identity is the forward-path within the open channel; 1463 scopes the decision 'to a particular address'.
