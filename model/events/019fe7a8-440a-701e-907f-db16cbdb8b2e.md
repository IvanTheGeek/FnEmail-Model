## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — the inherited MessageRelayedToNextHop notes named this arm as real and dropped it 'only for space', which is precisely the truncation the re-sweep exists to undo. Strength: MAY, one of three mutually exclusive arms of a single sentence. It sits in THIS chapter rather than the final-delivery chapter because of 3913-3915: choosing this arm cancels every sorting, self-match and truncation rule in §5.1, so the disposition is the routing decision. Siblings: MessageRelayedByDesignatedExchanger, MessageHandedOffOutsideSmtpEnvironment. Rejected alternative names: FinalDeliveryMadeByMailExchanger, MessageDeliveredLocallyByExchanger. Which project screen would have killed it: the responsibility-boundary cutoff, which is exactly why the three-way fork is worth surfacing intact. ⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck message_id (example \<f2C8D14@foo.com>), on the same grounds as its sibling arms: RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier, §4.4's ID clause is optional and belongs to the receiving host's trace field (3329, 3336-3337), §6.4 line 4131 shows a message may carry none, and f2C8D14 appears nowhere in the RFC — it is this project's own minted queue-id value. Identity is the forward-path delivered and the host that delivered it; both come from D.3.
