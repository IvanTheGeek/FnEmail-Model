## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. The raw set carried only the determination (ReturnAddressDeterminedInvalid); the act the determination authorizes had no slot. It is a separate state change and an irreversible one: a deliverable message is destroyed. Strength: "it may be a rational policy" (4045-4046) is not a MAY — it is an observation about what a site might rationally choose — and the RFC argues against it in the same sentence at 4047-4049, which is unusual enough to be worth recording verbatim. Distinct from MessageSilentlyDropped, and NOT merged with it: that one turns on a content judgment ("seriously fraudulent or otherwise inappropriate") and this one on an envelope judgment about the return path, and the two paragraphs of §6.2 keep them apart. Whether the sender is notified is not stated for this case at all, which is a genuine gap: the drop is a non-delivery, so §6.1's MUST would seem to apply, except that the very address a notification would go to is the one just judged invalid — a hotspot, and arguably the reason the RFC left it unsaid. Rejected alternative names: MailWithInvalidReturnAddressDiscarded, InvalidSenderPolicyApplied, ReturnAddressDropExecuted. Which screens would have killed it: the consumer test (G1), the inbound-only scope where policy drops are out of frame, and the altitude tiers.

=== FIELD HYGIENE PASS === Struck one of three fields: message_ref (f2C8D14), fabricated — RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier. WHAT CARRIES IDENTITY HERE INSTEAD: the reverse-path / forward-path pair, which is the whole handle this chapter has for a message once the minted key is gone. Both surviving fields are recorded facts and neither is derived: the address judged invalid is the cause, and the recipient who never gets the message is the cost the RFC itself argues about at rfc5321.txt:4047-4049. Example corrected: reverse_path read \<Jones@foo.com>, the walk scene's recipient; the return address is \<Smith@bar.com> and the intended recipient is \<Jones@foo.com>.
