# `ConnectionFailureTreatedAsTransientError`

The client treated a transaction lost to a connection failure as though a 451 had been received, so the mail is retried rather than bounced.

## Where RFC 5321 says so

| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| §3.8 | 1671-1676 | *"SMTP clients that experience a connection close, reset, or other communications failure due to circumstances not under their control (in violation of the intent of this specification but sometimes unavoidable) SHOULD, to maintain the robustness of the mail system, treat the mail transaction as if a 451 response had been received and act accordingly."* |
| §4.1.1.10 | 2218-2219 | *"generally MUST act as if the command or transaction in progress had received a temporary error (i.e., a 4yz response)"* |

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP, and the clearest single measurement of what the 32-event cap cost. The inherited ConnectionLost candidate quoted the FIRST HALF of this sentence — up to the closing parenthesis — and stopped, losing the entire normative arm that follows: the SHOULD, the 451, and 'act accordingly'. The subordinate clause was captured and the main clause was not. It is the client-side twin of ConnectionLossTreatedAsQuit, and the RFC gives the two symmetric SHOULDs in different sections, which is why neither sweep saw both. This event is what makes a lost connection a retry rather than a bounce, so it is the entry point into the Queue chapter: what a queue entry is later read against is an ASSUMED 451, not a reply anyone sent. assumed_reply_code is a fact about the client's own decision, not an observation of the wire — no 451 ever arrived, which is what makes the field interesting rather than redundant. stream_root is Transaction rather than Session because what is reclassified is the mail transaction, and the session is already gone. Screens: the inbound-only scope and the responsibility-boundary cutoff. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck 2 fields. session_id struck: no such key in the spec, and RFC 5321 supplies no transaction identifier either, so this event's stream has no key at all — what names the transaction is its reverse-path and forward-paths, and what names this decision is the failure it answers. ⚠️ assumed_reply_code struck, against the note above, which is the strongest argument for a reply code anywhere in the chapter: §3.8 line 1675 does put the 451 inside the client rather than on the wire. It goes because it is constant — every occurrence is 451, fixed by that same line — so it restates the event's own name and meaning rather than recording anything that varies.
