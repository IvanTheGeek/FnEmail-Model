## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. The inherited set carried 7.8's detection and its close but not its OUTCOME — and 4342-4346 names two distinct attack purposes, of which only the first, address discovery, produced any candidate. Doubtful and kept per the instruction to include when unsure: a server becoming inaccessible is unquestionably a state change and is the one 7.8 exists to license defense against, but the RFC gives it no actor-side act and no reply, so it may be a consequence rather than an event. Strength: descriptive; 7.8 has no 2119 keywords. Rejected names: DenialOfServiceAchieved, ServiceDeniedToOthers, ServerOverwhelmed. stream_root is uncomfortable: the state changed belongs to a SERVICE as other users experience it, which no offered root names; an invented 'Service Availability' root would fit better. Cross-reference: ServiceUnavailableAnnounced and ServiceShutdownAnnounced are the announced form of unavailability; this one is never announced, which is the difference. Screens that would have killed it: all of them. FIELD HYGIENE: struck attack_purpose. Its value quoted 4344-4345 accurately, but the purpose is fixed by the event's own name — this IS the denial-of-service arm — so the field carried nothing, and the OTHER purpose at 4342-4344, discovery of addresses for unsolicited messages, already has its own event in AddressesHarvestedFromMailingList. Identity: RFC 5321 gives none here at all; the server that became inaccessible is the subject, and the attacking address is the peer identity derived from the TCP connection (4.4 lines 3325-3327), which is also the only handle 7.8 leaves a defender. Confidence unchanged: the doubt was always that the RFC gives this outcome no act and no reply, and striking a quoted-but-empty field neither deepens nor lifts it.
