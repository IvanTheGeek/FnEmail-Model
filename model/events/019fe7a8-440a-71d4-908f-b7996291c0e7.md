# `MalformedMessageRejected`

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — candidate 287's notes named it as a sibling carrying no normative strength and gave it no row. It is one of the three arms of the §6.4 debate and this chapter's brief names it explicitly ("malformed message tolerated or rejected"), so all three are given rows: this one, MalformedMessagePassedOnUnchanged, and the repair events above. NO NORMATIVE STRENGTH AT ALL is the finding, not a defect in the harvest: the RFC describes a debate that "shows no signs of abating" and declines to settle it, so an implementation may legitimately choose any arm and the choice is a policy fact the model must carry rather than derive. That makes the fork a hotspot in the strict sense — an open question the spec leaves open. Deliberately NOT merged with MessageRejectedAtEndOfData (raw candidate 180) or with the trace-header case: a refusal for message FORMAT is a different determination from a refusal for policy or resources, even when the wire result is the same 554. Rejected alternative name: IllFormedMessageRefused.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS (chapter 14): session_id struck — RFC 5321 names no session, transaction or connection identifier, and f2C8D14 is nowhere in the RFC; identity here is the message being refused, on this hop, which section 4.4's By-domain clause at line 3319 is the RFC's own way of recording. malformation KEPT and sourced to line 4133, since a missing origination date is one of the three irregularities section 6.4 enumerates. rejecting_host KEPT, with its value corrected from mx.bar.com, absent from RFC 5321, to bar.com, which Appendix D names as a host at 4881 and its trace line uses at 5064.
