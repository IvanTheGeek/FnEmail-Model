## Notes

Kept separate from DataCommandRejectedOutOfSequence deliberately: a refusal for recipient policy is not a refusal for a missing envelope, and merging them would lose the reason. Strength is unusually weak — "it would be reasonable to" (3605-3606), weaker than MAY in wording though it sits inside a MAY sentence. Two variants the RFC names in one breath: 503 after a prior 452 or 552, and 503 after DATA "without returning any previous negative response" (3607-3608); the second means every RCPT looked accepted and the refusal lands only at DATA, a materially different client experience that may deserve its own event. The recipient-limit machinery itself (§4.5.3.1.8, §4.5.3.1.10) belongs to the Mail Transaction chapter; only the DATA-time refusal is claimed here. Rejected alternative name: DataRefusedForRecipientPolicy. Screens: nothing kills it; it is a second refusal event in a transaction the twelve-slice model treats as having one.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck all four fields, the heaviest strip in the chapter relative to what was there. session_id is fabricated; identity is the channel and the position of the refused DATA. reply_code (503) and prior_overflow_reply_code (452) are renderings of decisions — the second is also a fact about an earlier event, recoverable from that event rather than restated here. recipients_accepted is a computed tally: it is a fold of the transaction's accepted RCPT events, which the method assigns to a read model, and §4.5.3.1.8's 100-recipient floor is a limit on the server, not a payload on this event. The event is now fieldless and the two variants named above are the only content it has — which is an argument for naming them as two events, not for restoring a field.
