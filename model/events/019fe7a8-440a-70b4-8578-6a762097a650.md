## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — the sweep recorded the MUST inside MailDataTransformedForLocalStorage and never named its violation, which is the arm with observable consequences. The pairing of the two citations is the argument: 3441-3443 requires every character, tabs and other control characters included, to reach the mailbox, so a transform that merely normalizes whitespace has already broken the rule without anyone noticing. Strength: MUST, violated. Doubtful because it is detectable only by comparing against a copy nobody keeps — which is precisely the hotspot: the reversibility requirement is unverifiable from inside the storing system, exactly as DuplicateMessageReceived is undetectable from inside the receiving one. Two systemic siblings on the same theme of silent content mutation, all three of which any model of content integrity has to hold together: HighOrderBitCleared (receiver), ExtraLineEndingAppended (sender), this one (store). Screens: all of them. Rejected alternative names: NonReversibleTransformApplied, ContentLostInStorageConversion.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck two of three. transaction_id is fabricated — no transaction key exists in RFC 5321, and a store is past the session; identity here is the queue id of the §4.4 ID clause where one was stamped, and nothing otherwise. characters_lost is struck as derived: "horizontal tabs normalized to spaces" already states which character was lost, so the second field recomputes the first. transform survives, and its value is anchored in the RFC's own list of what must survive to the mailbox at rfc5321.txt:3443 ("spaces, vertical and horizontal tabs, and other control characters") — which is what makes an apparently harmless whitespace transform a violation.
