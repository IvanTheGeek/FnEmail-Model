## Notes

Real data is the RFC's OWN failing recipient: Appendix D.1 says Green does not have a mailbox at host foo.com and answers RCPT TO:\<Green@foo.com> with 550 No such user here — so TWO reason-text forms exist in the document, and §3.3's form is quoted here per rule 2, trailing space and all. 'other circumstances and reply codes are possible' explicitly refuses to close the set, so reply_code has a real range. 'is known not to be a deliverable address' is a PRECONDITION — something earlier made addresses deliverable or not, which points at a Mailbox-rooted provisioning event outside this chapter. The reason text leaks mailbox existence, in tension with the VRFY disclosure discussion in §3.5.3 and with RecipientRejectedWithoutAddressInformation below, which is the same refusal deliberately saying nothing. Rejected alternative names: RecipientUnknown, NoSuchUserReported.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck two fields. reply_code (550) goes as the wire rendering of a decision; the RFC's own sentence at 1071-1074 supplies it and explicitly refuses to close the set ('other circumstances and reply codes are possible'), which is a statement about renderings, not about state. reason_text ('no such user - Green@foo.com') goes for the reason the note itself supplies: the document carries TWO different forms for this same refusal, §3.3's 'no such user - ' plus the mailbox name and Appendix D.1's 550 No such user here at rfc5321.txt:4898, and a fact that the source states two ways is operator rendering, not payload.
