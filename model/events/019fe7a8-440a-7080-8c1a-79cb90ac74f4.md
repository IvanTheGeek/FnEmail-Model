## Notes

Carried unmerged. rejected_argument is real and deliberate: mail_server.bar.com contains an underscore, which §4.1.2 forbids in a domain label, so it is the concrete case that earns the 501 — and §4.1.2 lines 2375-2378 make that rejection a MUST in its own right. RFC INCONSISTENCY FOUND IN THE RE-SWEEP and carried forward: §4.1.4 lines 2461-2462 enumerates 501, 500, 502, 550 as the failure replies, while the §4.3.2 sequence table at lines 3096-3099 gives 504, 550, 502 for the same command; the two lists do not agree, neither is marked illustrative, and 501 and 500 are missing from the table while 504 is missing from the prose. The fact a later step must not lose: a REJECTED mid-session greeting does not clear the buffers, while an ACCEPTED one clears everything (§4.1.4 lines 2453-2456), so a rejected EHLO issued during an open transaction leaves that transaction open — and nothing in the spec says whether the client is expected to know that. Both arms are MUST. Screens: the consumer test G1 — on the server side literally nothing changes, so a strict read rejects it; it is still an event for the client, whose next legal move changed. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck 2 fields. session_id struck — no such key in the spec; identity is the greeting being answered and its position in the session. reply_code 501 struck as the rendering of the refusal, and the strike sharpens rather than hides the inconsistency recorded above: the RFC gives two disagreeing lists of permitted codes for this one refusal, which is a finding about the source and stays in prose where a reader will meet it, not a payload value picked from one of the two lists. rejected_argument survives as the fact that earns the rejection; its value is not in the RFC but the rule it breaks is, at lines 2372-2374.
