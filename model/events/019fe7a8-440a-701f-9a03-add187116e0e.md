## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — third arm of the same MAY, also dropped for space by the inherited sweep. §3.6.3 lines 1490-1492 is what makes it this chapter's rather than the Gatewaying chapter's: MX records themselves can point at gateways, so this is a next-hop outcome selected by the same lookup, and like the final-delivery arm it cancels further MX examination (3913-3915). The mechanism is unspecified by construction — 'some mechanism outside the SMTP-provided transport environment' names nothing: 🟥, and the handoff_mechanism field is therefore an open payload. What the gateway then DOES (header translation, envelope reconstruction, address rewriting) belongs to the Gatewaying chapter; only the handoff is here. Rejected alternative names: MessageHandedToGateway (narrower than the RFC's wording — not every non-SMTP mechanism is a gateway), MessageLeftSmtpEnvironment. Which project screen would have killed it: the responsibility-boundary cutoff and the inbound-only scope. ⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck message_id (example \<f2C8D14@foo.com>), on the same grounds as its two sibling arms: RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier, §4.4's ID clause is optional and is the receiving host's trace entry (3329, 3336-3337), §6.4 line 4131 proves a message may carry no message-id at all, and f2C8D14 is this project's own minted value found nowhere in the RFC. Identity is the handing host and the handoff itself. handoff_mechanism keeps its illustrative label honestly: 3912-3913 names no mechanism, which is the 🟥 already recorded above, so no RFC line can source the value.
