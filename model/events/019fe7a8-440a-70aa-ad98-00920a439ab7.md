## Data

**No data.** The method sanctions this: *"If an event has no meaningful payload beyond its identity (e.g., a simple state transition), it is fine to have no fields or just the identity key."* It also warns, of a different case, that *"An event without fields is an opaque label"* — both are recorded here rather than reconciled, because reconciling them is a later step's work, not Brain Storming's.

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP; the sweeps quoted the MUST NOT but attached it to EndOfMailDataIndicated rather than naming the violation. Strength: MUST NOT, with exactly one stated exception, which is the subject of TerminatingLineEndingAdded and MessageRejectedAsInvalidByOriginator below — so the three events together are the complete decision the RFC lays out for a body whose final line lacks \<CRLF>. The consequence is silent and permanent: the message gains a blank line that no one asked for and nothing removes, which is a content mutation by the SENDER, the mirror of HighOrderBitCleared's mutation by the receiver. Doubtful only because it is a violation and therefore never emitted by a conforming client. The second quote is deliberately truncated mid-sentence as the RFC's line 2014-2015 stands, because the sentence continues across a page break at 2016-2022; rule 2 forbids tidying it. Screens: the altitude tiers and the inbound-only scope. Rejected alternative name: SpuriousCrlfAddedBeforeTerminator.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck both fields. transaction_id is fabricated — RFC 5321 names no transaction identifier; identity is the channel and the position of the terminator. added_sequence (\<CRLF>) is struck as a constant: the RFC's rule is about one specific sequence, so the field holds the same value on every instance and restates the event's meaning rather than describing an occurrence. Fieldless, and the loss is nil — the mutation is fully named by the event.
