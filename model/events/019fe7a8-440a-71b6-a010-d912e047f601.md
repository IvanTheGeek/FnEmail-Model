## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP as a split from ReturnPathVerified, which had folded both verdicts into a Boolean field. The two outcomes have different consequences - a failed verification is the input to withholding a notification, and withholding is itself governed by the MUST at 1496-1499 - so collapsing them hides a conflict between a MAY and a MUST. HOTSPOT in its own right: 3.6.3 says a relay MUST send an undeliverable notification to the address in the reverse-path; 3.6.2 says the server MAY first decide that address is not to be trusted; nothing in the RFC says what happens when both apply. Strength: MAY, with no method defined and none recommended. Rejected names: ReturnPathRejected, ReversePathVerificationFailed, ReturnPathDistrusted. Screens: consumer test, two-kinds lens. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck checking_host - the recorder's own domain, and mx.bar.com has no source in the RFC. The two survivors match ReturnPathVerified exactly, which is what a split pair should look like: same subject, opposite verdict, no extra payload smuggled onto either arm.
