| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| §7.1 | 4186-4192 | *"Efforts to make it more difficult for users to set envelope return path and header "From" fields to point to valid addresses other than their own are largely misguided: they frustrate legitimate applications in which mail is sent by one user on behalf of another, in which error (or normal) replies should be directed to a special address, or in which a single message is sent to multiple recipients on different hosts."* |

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. This is the event 7.1 defends, and the one that makes MessageSpoofedToAppearFromElsewhere undetectable in principle: the two are the SAME wire act with opposite intent and no field distinguishes them — the chapter's central hotspot, and the RFC's own position rather than an inference. Strength: none; 'largely misguided' (4188) is editorial. Rejected names: ReturnPathDelegated, SenderSetOnBehalfOfAnother, ThirdPartyReturnPathDeclared. Collides with this project's settled ReversePathDeclared: same declaration, but 7.1's point is whose address it may be. Screens that would have killed it: inbound-only scope, responsibility-boundary cutoff. FIELD HYGIENE: nothing struck — the rare record whose fields all hold. purpose is not invented: 4189-4192 enumerates three legitimate purposes and the example is the first, at line 4189, so the field is enumerable from the spec. The two mailboxes are walk-scene values. No identity key was fabricated and none is needed: the declaration is carried by the reverse path itself.
