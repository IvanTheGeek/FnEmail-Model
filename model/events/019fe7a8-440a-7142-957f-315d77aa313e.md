## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP of the blind-copy material. The inherited set carried the two consequences of this step (BccHeaderFieldRemoved, EmptyBccHeaderFieldInserted) and the general copy-generation event, but not the BCC-specific copy that creates the blind copy in the first place — which is the moment the address changes layers, from header to envelope, and the moment §7.2's whole concern begins. Strength: SHOULD, and the ordering with respect to removal is fixed by the spec and load-bearing: copy first (4737-4739), remove after (4740-4741); reversed, the recipients are lost. Rejected alternative names: RecipientAddressCopiedToRcptCommand, BccAddressPromotedToEnvelope, BlindCopyEnvelopeRecipientCreated. Deliberately narrowed to the BCC arm: the TO and CC arms of the same sentence are ordinary envelope construction and belong with EnvelopeGeneratedFromHeaderFields and MessageCopyGenerated, which are recorded in set_aside as belonging to whichever chapter takes Appendix B — no chapter in the sixteen names it. The "group" clause at 4739-4740 is a third expansion mechanism, at submission, from RFC 822 syntax rather than from an alias table; noted rather than made an event because it is address syntax, not a stored list. Screens: inbound-only scope, responsibility-boundary cutoff. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: source_header_field struck — 1 field. It could only ever hold "BCC" on an event named BlindCopyRecipientCopiedFromBccHeaderField, so it restated the name and carried no information; a constant is padding by the method's own rule, whatever line it can be traced to. Nothing else went: blind_copy_recipient is the address that changed layers, which is the fact of the event, and submission_host says which of the scene's two hosts acted, which matters here because the chapter's blind-copy events span both ends of the wire. Identity: none available and none invented — no transaction identifier exists in RFC 5321, and at submission time nothing has been accepted anywhere, so no §4.4 ID clause has been stamped either.
