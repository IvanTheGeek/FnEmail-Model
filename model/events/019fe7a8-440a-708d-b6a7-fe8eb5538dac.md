## Notes

One event with a phase field covering four subsections — MAIL 5 minutes, RCPT 5 minutes, DATA initiation 2 minutes awaiting the 354, data block 3 minutes per TCP SEND. Splitting into four is defensible and a facilitator may well demand it: the data-block timer is per-buffer and can fire many times in one transaction, which no other member of the group does. The RCPT entry carries a hidden dependency worth keeping — a longer timeout is required if list and alias processing is not deferred until after acceptance, which names mailing-list expansion and alias resolution as things happening INSIDE the RCPT window and implies list events on the receiving side (List chapter). All values are SHOULD floors. DATA termination is deliberately excluded from this group — see DataTerminationTimeoutExpired, held out because its consequence is categorically different. Also folded here and not carried as its own event, cut for the cap: CommandTimerStarted, the arming of the per-command timer (§4.5.3.2 lines 3615-3616), which is where the spec's observation that 'the overall timeout is inherently proportional to the size of the message' comes from; it was marked doubtful in the raw set as close to the read/lookup line, and the altitude tiers kill it cleanly as Infrastructure. Screens: inbound-only scope. FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck 3 fields. session_id struck: no such key in the spec; identity is the channel and the phase it died in. waited_seconds struck as site configuration copied onto the event, recorded at TimeoutPolicyConfigured. minimum_seconds struck as derived twice over: it is a constant per phase, so it is a function of the phase field beside it, and the constants are the RFC's own subsection headings at lines 3631, 3633, 3647 and 3651 rather than anything this event observes. phase survives as the one fact that varies, and it is exactly what the four subsections index.
