## Notes

Merged ConnectionLost (§3.8 sweep) and ConnectionLostUnexpectedly (§4.1.1.x sweep) — the same loss observed from the two ends. The two ends' REACTIONS are deliberately kept as separate events, ConnectionLossTreatedAsQuit and ConnectionFailureTreatedAsTransientError, because the spec gives them separate SHOULDs in separate sections and a party may honor one without the other existing. Rejected alternative names: ConnectionLost, ConnectionLostUnexpectedly, TransportFailureDetected, ConnectionDropped. failure_kind takes its three values straight from the RFC's own enumeration — close, reset, other — worth preserving because the model's TransactionAborted{cause: rset \| quit \| disconnect} collapses all three into 'disconnect'. last_command_sent is what decides whether the message may already have been delivered: lost after the final dot but before the 250 is the duplicate-delivery case §4.5.3.2.6 warns about. The actor is neither SMTP party, which makes this the only event of its kind in the chapter, and it is the counterexample to any model assuming every session ends with QUIT. The parenthetical is unusual RFC prose and worth quoting whole: it concedes that a conforming implementation will meet nonconforming behavior routinely. Screens: the altitude tiers rule a transport reset Infrastructure; the consumer test bites if nothing drawn reads it.
