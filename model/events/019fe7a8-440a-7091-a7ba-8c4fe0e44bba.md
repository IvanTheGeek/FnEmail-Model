# `ConnectionLossTreatedAsQuit`

The server treated a dropped or reset connection as though a QUIT had arrived before the connection disappeared.

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP, as a split out of the inherited ConnectionLostUnexpectedly, which folded the detection and the interpretation into one event and asked in its own notes whether they were one or two. They are two, and the split is load-bearing rather than pedantic: there are TWO SHOULDs here, not MUSTs — be prepared for the condition, and treat it as a QUIT — so a server may decline the interpretation and leave a different aftermath, which is precisely the arm a fused event cannot express. Rejected alternative name from the merge: ConnectionLossTreatedAsQuit was already named in the raw set's notes as a folded sibling and is promoted here. Its consequence, carried in the Transaction chapter, is stated in half a line at §4.1.1.10 and is the durability boundary of the whole protocol: the server MUST cancel the pending transaction but MUST NOT undo any completed one, so a message already answered with 250 stays accepted even if the connection dies a second later. Note the framing: the spec describes a condition 'contrary to the intent of this specification' and nonetheless requires handling for it — the robustness principle showing through.
