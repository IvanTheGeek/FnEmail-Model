## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — named in the inherited SelfIdentifiedInMailExchangerList notes and again in the span notes as precondition (b), and never given a slot in either sweep. No rule in RFC 5321 establishes it; the spec presupposes the list exists and is complete. Its incompleteness is precisely what generates the loops §6.3 then has to detect, which makes this the highest-leverage unstated precondition in the chapter. Strength: none — the MUST is on the inspection, not on the configuration. Nothing beyond the identity and the two lists is carried: the risk here is padding, not fieldlessness. Over-time siblings: ServerIdentityNameAdded, ServerIdentityNameRemoved — the removal is what silently reopens a loop. Rejected alternative names: RelayKnownNamesConfigured, SelfIdentitySetDefined. Which project screen would have killed it: the four altitude tiers file configuration outside the protocol tier, and the consumer test has nothing to consume it. ⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS: nothing struck; the inherited notes had already applied the anti-padding test and the result holds. Both lists are named in the RFC's own sentence at 3929-3930 ('any of the names or addresses'), so neither is invented and neither is derivable from the other. No fabricated identity key was present; identity is the relay host whose configuration this is.
