## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP. §7.7 is three lines long and the merged list took only its provisioning presupposition (ReplacementAddressAssociatedWithMailbox) and its configuration remedy — nothing carried the harm itself, which is the section's actual subject and the reason §3.4 spends its whole length arguing for suppression. The state change is real and irreversible: information that was private is now held by a party outside, and no later configuration change can retract it. Kept distinct from CorrectedDestinationDisclosed, which is the same wire act seen as protocol; this is the same act seen as a privacy fact about the MAILBOX, hence the different stream root — and the pair is a good test of whether one wire act may legitimately raise two events. Strength: "may inadvertently disclose" — no 2119 keyword; §7.7's only instruction is a lowercase "should" at 4336-4338. Marked likely because whether a given disclosure was inadvertent is not knowable from the reply alone. Rejected alternative names: ReplacementAddressLeaked, ForwardingRelationshipExposed. Screens: inbound-only scope, the consumer test, and the twelve-slice model — but note the exposure is exactly what §3.4 and §7.7 both exist to prevent, so a model that cannot express it cannot express the section's purpose.

⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS (fields and notes only): struck one field, reply_code (251) — the rendering of a decision, and §7.7 rfc5321.txt:4334-4336 names "the 251 or 551 reply codes" as one case, so which code carried the leak is not the privacy fact. What survives IS the privacy fact, in three parts: whose address, what was revealed, and to whom. disclosed_to is a claimed domain in the same way announcing_server is on the §7.4 sibling — a server learns the peer's name from EHLO and RFC 5321 gives it no way to verify it — so the field records who said they were listening, not who was, and a disclosure event that cannot name its true recipient is a sharper finding than one that pretends to. Sourced: mailbox at rfc5321.txt:4897; disclosed_to at rfc5321.txt:4887, the RFC's own EHLO bar.com; disclosed_replacement composed from the RFC's Green (4884) and xyz.com (1940). Note the pairing with CorrectedDestinationDisclosed survives the pass and is now cleaner: after both lost their reply_code, the two events differ by stream root and by which facts they carry, which is the honest form of the question the notes above raise about one wire act raising two events.
