## Notes

Harvested by meaning from a §7.3 sweep, not from this chapter's anchors: §7.3 justifies keeping VRFY and EXPN by the internal audit they enable, and that audit only makes sense if forwarding rules exist and can be created by users. The event is implied entirely by the check being worth running; the RFC never specifies its creation. Kept separate from ForwardingAliasProvisioned on actor and on trust: an administrator consolidating addresses and a user redirecting sensitive mail off-site are the same shape with opposite intent, and §7.3 treats the second as something to hunt for. Strength: none — prose, no 2119 keyword. The audit itself is a read and is set aside. Opposite arm not stated: the RFC never describes revoking such a rule. Screens: inbound-only scope, responsibility-boundary cutoff, and the consumer test (G1) all fire here, and all three are the wrong tool at Step 1.

⚠️ FIELD HYGIENE PASS (fields and notes only): struck one field, created_by (Admin.MRC). The mailbox owner is already named twice over, by source_mailbox and by the actor, so the field added a third rendering of one fact. No identity key was added; the rule is identified by the mailbox it redirects. Sourced: source_mailbox at rfc5321.txt:5114, forward_to at rfc5321.txt:4893 — both are the RFC's own addresses, and the pairing of an internal Admin.MRC mailbox with an outside bar.com destination is what makes the §7.3 audit case concrete.
