## Notes

Carried from #84. The trigger for this chapter's strongest MUST. Two grounds are stated - 'the destination is incorrect' and 'cannot be delivered for some other reason' - recorded as ONE event with a reason field rather than two, because the RFC joins them with 'or' into a single condition and the obligation that follows is identical; a reader who wants them split should split on reason and cite the same lines. 'the host that first determines' (1504-1505) presupposes that the determination is a datable act attributable to one host - exactly this event, and the hook that decides who owes the notification. Strength: the MUST sits on the consequence (constructing and sending), not on the determination. Rejected names: NonDeliveryConcluded, DeliveryAbandoned, DestinationFoundIncorrect. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck determined_at (event time restated). queue_id kept on the 4.4 ID clause, as in DeliveryAttemptFailed. reason kept and now sourced: the value quotes the RFC's own ground at 1495, so it is a recorded ground rather than a computed status - that distinction is what the derived-value rule turns on.
