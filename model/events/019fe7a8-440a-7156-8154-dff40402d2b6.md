## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP as the honored arm of the principle that governs this entire chapter — every suppression, drop and bounce in §6.2 is stated as a departure from it, so the model needs the baseline the departures depart from. Strength: lowercase "should be delivered" (4016), a principle rather than a requirement, and the RFC withdraws its own support two sentences later at 4021-4025 ("those principles may not be practical"). Confidence is doubtful and the reason is recorded rather than hidden: this may be better read as an ordinary successful delivery carrying a fault_tolerated field, in which case it belongs to the final-delivery chapter and not here — the only thing that makes it a distinct state change is that a fault was noticed and the message was delivered in spite of it, and "noticed" is not something the RFC records. Kept because deleting it would leave §6.2's three refusal events (silent drop, invalid-return-address drop, hostile-content suppression) with nothing to be exceptions to. Overlaps §6.4 Compensating for Irregularities (heading verified at line 4074), which belongs to another chapter and is where a repair-rather-than-tolerate event would live. Rejected alternative names: MessageDeliveredDespiteFaults, DeliveryPrincipleHonored, FaultTolerantDeliveryMade. Which screens would have killed it: the consumer test (G1) and the inbound-only scope.

=== FIELD HYGIENE PASS === Struck one of three fields: message_ref (f2C8D14), fabricated — RFC 5321 supplies no message identifier. WHAT CARRIES IDENTITY HERE INSTEAD: the forward-path the message was delivered to. Example corrected: recipient read \<Smith@bar.com>, which is the SENDER in both the walked path and Appendix D (rfc5321.txt:4893); the recipient is \<Jones@foo.com>. fault_tolerated survives because it is the entire distinction between this event and an ordinary delivery, but it is now marked illustrative: the RFC names the category ("any syntax or other faults", rfc5321.txt:4016-4017) and no instance of it. CONFIDENCE UNCHANGED at doubtful — it was already doubtful before this pass, for the reason recorded above, and nothing found here strengthens or weakens that argument; the record's own point that "noticed" is not something the RFC records stands untouched.
