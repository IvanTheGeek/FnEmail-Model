## Notes

MERGED from two raw candidates: NotificationSuppressedToPreventLoop (§3.6.3) and the §4.5.4 half of ErrorNotificationSuppressedToPreventLoop. The merge is safe because both are unconditional MUST NOTs on the same act — do not report the failure of a report — and §4.5.4 adds "under any circumstances", which is as absolute as this RFC gets. The other half of that raw candidate, the §4.5.5 SHOULD NOT about automated processors replying to a null-reverse-path message, was NOT merged in: it is a different strength, a different actor, and a different act (a reply, not an error report), and it is carried by AutoReplySentToNullReversePathMessage below. That split is the single clearest example in the chapter of the brief's warning not to merge things that merely look alike. Suppression is a real state change because the message is now dropped and no one will be told — the failure is deliberately made silent, and that silence is a fact worth recording even though the RFC asks no one to record it. Paired with NullReversePathSet as the two independent loop-prevention mechanisms: one structural, one by rule. Opposite event, recovered below: NotificationSentAboutNotificationFailure. Rejected alternative names: ErrorNotificationSuppressedToPreventLoop, BounceOfBounceSuppressed, LoopBreakApplied. Which screens would have killed it: the consumer test (G1) — nothing consumes a non-send — plus the capture/decision two-kinds lens and the inbound-only scope.
