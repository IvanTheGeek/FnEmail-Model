Distinct from a rejection during the transaction, and the custody line is the ONLY thing that separates them — the same failure, on either side of the 250, produces either DeliveryResponsibilityDeclinedTemporarily or this event plus a mandatory notification. That is worth drawing as a pair on the wall; it is the single sharpest illustration of what the handoff actually buys. stream_root is Recipient rather than Message because the failure is per-recipient even when the acceptance was per-message — the asymmetry PartialDeliveryFailureDetected makes explicit. The RFC concedes at 4000-4001 that this case cannot be engineered away. Its example is a reply text and is sourced as such — line 2830, the 550 text — because the RFC offers no other vocabulary for a delivery failure at this point.

COLLISION RESOLVED — merged from 2 independently brainstormed records. Both records rest on the same primary sentence, 6.1 lines 3970-3971, and describe the same fact: a party that had already taken custody determines the message cannot be delivered. The actors are not in conflict — chapter 12's ("receiver-SMTP or relay SMTP server holding custody, the host that first determines delivery is impossible") is a superset of chapter 5's, and it is a superset the RFC itself authorizes at 3.6.3 lines 1503-1505, which attaches the obligation to "the host that first determines that delivery cannot be accomplished" whatever role that host occupies. One datable determination: merge. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the UndeliverableMailNotificationSent resolution below, where the two records are kept apart — the RFC unifies the DETERMINER in one sentence, but states the SENDING obligations in two places, for two roles, with different normative content.

Non-Delivery Notification: MERGED from three raw candidates naming the same state change from two role vantages: DeliveryFailedAfterAcceptance (§6.1, receiver-SMTP after the 250) and DeliveryDeterminedImpossible (§3.6.3, relay that accepted the relaying task). Rejected alternative names carried here so the naming research survives: DeliveryDeterminedImpossible, MessageReturnedAsNonDeliverable (rejected — that names the send, not the failure), DeliveryFailureDiagnosed, RecipientFailedAfterDataAccepted. The merge is defensible because §3.6.3 line 1503-1505 explicitly makes the obligation attach to "the host that first determines that delivery cannot be accomplished" — one datable determination, whatever role the host occupies. Strength: the MUST is on the consequence (formulate and mail / construct and send), not on the determination itself; the determination is a precondition the RFC presupposes. §6.1 lines 4001-4006 supplies three named non-frivolous causes and they are the realistic values of failure_reason: a "soft" domain system error, the target being a mailing list, and the server acting as a relay with no immediate access to the delivering system. Opposite event: DeliveryResponsibilityAccepted (§6.1 3962-3964), which belongs to the acceptance chapter and is the custody line this whole chapter hangs from. Sibling not merged: DeliveryGivenUp (§4.5.4.1 3709-3711), retry exhaustion — a different trigger on a Queue Entry, cross-referenced but left to the queue/retry chapter.
