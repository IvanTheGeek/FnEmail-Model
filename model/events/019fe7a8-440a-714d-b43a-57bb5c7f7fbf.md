## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — the violated arm of the chapter's most absolute MUST NOT, and precisely the kind of event the 32-event cap cut off, since every sweep that reached these lines recorded only the honored arm. The RFC states the prohibition twice and in the second place adds "under any circumstances", which tells you it is violated in practice; a prohibition nobody breaks does not get restated with an intensifier. The consequence is the bounce loop the null reverse-path exists to make impossible, and it is observable: the same pair of hosts exchanging notifications about notifications. Confidence is likely, not certain, because the RFC never describes the violation happening — it is inferred from the shape of the prohibition, exactly as the brief's two-arms instruction directs. Opposite event: NotificationSuppressedToPreventLoop. Rejected alternative names: BounceLoopStarted (rejected — it names the aggregate consequence, not the single act), NotificationOfNotificationSent, ErrorMessageSentInResponseToErrorMessage. Which screens would have killed it: every one of them — a non-conforming act has no command, no consumer, and no slice; the inbound-only scope and the responsibility-boundary cutoff put it outside twice over.

=== FIELD HYGIENE PASS === Struck two of three fields. (1) subject_notification_ref (f2C8D14-ndn1) — fabricated; RFC 5321 gives a notification no identifier. (2) sending_host — struck as a restatement of the event-level actor. WHAT CARRIES IDENTITY HERE INSTEAD: only the address the forbidden second notification was aimed at, which is the surviving field and the single observable of the loop. Example corrected to the walk scene's originator, \<Smith@bar.com>; it read \<Jones@foo.com>, the recipient. Sourcing note, stated plainly because item 5 says an unsourceable value is a signal: the RFC states NO recipient rule for an act it forbids, so this value is walk scene and not RFC-derived, and a facilitator who finds one walk-scene address too thin should let this event go fieldless like its opposite arm.
