# `ExistingReceivedLineAlteredByGateway`

A gateway changed a Received: line that was already in the header section — typically "fixing" one that arrived nonconforming from a foreign environment — destroying the trace evidence that line carried.

## Where RFC 5321 says so

| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| §3.7.2 | 1577-1579 | *"When forwarding a message into or out of the Internet environment, a gateway MUST prepend a Received: line, but it MUST NOT alter in any way a Received: line that is already in the header section."* |
| §3.7.2 | 1581-1582 | *"Received:" header fields of messages originating from other environments may not conform exactly to this specification."* |
| §3.7.2 | 1583-1585 | *"the most important use of Received: lines is for debugging mail faults, and this debugging can be severely hampered by well-meaning gateways that try to "fix" a Received: line."* |

## Notes

RECOVERED BY THE RE-SWEEP — the capped sweep named it "ExistingReceivedLineAltered" in the fold list and gave it no slot. This is the VIOLATED ARM of a MUST NOT, and the RFC is unusual in naming the offender's motive: "well-meaning gateways that try to 'fix' a Received: line" (lines 1584-1585). That motive is why the violation happens in practice and why it survives review, so it belongs on the wall. Cross-reference, deliberately NOT merged: ReceivedLineChainAltered in the trace chapter cites §4.4 lines 3178-3182, the general integrity rule for any server; this event is the gateway-specific case where the alteration is a repair of a foreign-format line the gateway had no standing to judge. A facilitator who wants one card should keep both citations and both motives. Rejected alternative names: ReceivedLineFixedByGateway, ForeignTraceLineNormalized, TraceLineRepaired. Which project screen would have killed it: inbound-only scope; the "one event per command" target has nowhere to put an act that happens between commands.

FIELD HYGIENE PASS: struck two fields, leaving identity only. (1) message_ref (example f2C8D14) — no such key in RFC 5321. (2) altered_line_origin_environment (example X.400) — unsourceable in two ways: the value cannot be sourced (X.400 appears at line 3466 as an address form, never as the provenance of a trace line), and more seriously the RFC gives the gateway no way to KNOW the origin environment of a line already in the header section — that is precisely what the missing via clause would have told it (see ViaClauseOmittedByGateway). A field the actor cannot populate is an invented field. What identifies the event instead: the gateway that altered the line, plus the trace chain the alteration damaged. Down to one field, and that is the correct outcome — the meaningful fact here is that the alteration happened at all.
