## Where RFC 5321 says so

| Section | Lines | Text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| §3.6.3 | 1524-1527 | *"a relay SMTP has no need to inspect or act upon the header section or body of the message data and MUST NOT do so except to add its own "Received:" header field (Section 4.4)"* |
| §4.4 | 3179-3182 | *"SMTP servers MUST prepend Received lines to messages; they MUST NOT change the order of existing lines or insert Received lines in any other location."* |

## Notes

Carried from #79; #43 (TraceInformationAdded), #178 (TraceRecordInserted) and #298 (TraceHeaderFieldPrepended) name the same state change from the trace chapter and are the rejected alternative names. Strength: this is a CARVE-OUT INSIDE A MUST NOT, a permission expressed negatively - the act is allowed precisely because everything else is forbidden. Worth stating that way on the wall, because the interesting constraint is the boundary, not the act. Citation nuance verified in the file: 3.6.3 says 'add'; 4.4 lines 3179-3182 are stricter about POSITION - MUST prepend, MUST NOT insert elsewhere - so a reader who cites only 3.6.3 under-states the obligation. --- FIELD HYGIENE: struck relay_host; the BY clause (By-domain, 3319) does carry it, but it is the recorder's own domain. stamped_at is the ONE timestamp kept in this chapter, because 4.4's Stamp grammar puts a date-time on the wire (3311-3313) and 3186-3188 lay rules on it (explicit offsets rather than time zone names) - it is data, not the event's own clock. Both values now come from the RFC's own relay trace line at 5064-5065, 'Received: from bar.com by foo.com ; Thu, 21 May 1998 05:33:29 -0700', which also shows the ID clause omitted.
